# Vocabulary

*Working vocabulary for articles and discussions in A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This file controls **wording** in expository prose: Medium articles, drafts, and extended discussions. Full definitions stay in `glossary.md`; this file does not repeat them. Each entry gives the term as used in prose, a plain gloss to use at first mention, the corresponding glossary term, and words to avoid as substitutes.

Drawn from "What Is Life?" and "The Chain of Life" (Medium, Nov 2026).

## Core terms

- **regeneration** — a living system rebuilding the means of its own continuation; "rebuilds itself." First mention in an article may use the full "organizational regeneration." *Glossary:* Organizational regeneration. *Avoid:* self-maintenance, self-repair, metabolism, homeostasis, autopoiesis (except when citing Maturana and Varela, then tie it to regeneration).
- **reproduction** — living systems making new living systems; "producing offspring." *Glossary:* Reproduction. *Avoid:* replication, self-replication, copying (keep "copying" for templates and for the replicator-first view).
- **heredity** — passing a parent's particular organization on to its offspring, so offspring resemble their parent more than other members of the population; "passing on traits." *Glossary:* Heredity. *Avoid:* inheritance as a synonym (in the glossary, inheritance is the *means* of heredity).
- **capability** (the three capabilities, the chain of life capabilities) — regeneration, reproduction, heredity. *Glossary:* Base capability. *Avoid:* function, property, feature, criterion (only regeneration is the criterion).
- **living system** — a system that regenerates its own organization. *Glossary:* Living system. *Avoid:* organism as a synonym (see below), life-form, entity.
- **organism** — a living system that belongs to an evolving lineage, whether or not it reproduces (a mule is one; the biosphere is not). *Glossary:* Organism.
- **lineage** — a line of living systems, each founded from one before it. *Glossary:* Lineage.
- **evolving lineage** — a lineage whose particular variations are carried forward to offspring. *Glossary:* Evolving lineage.

## Continuation and renewal

- **continuation principle** — anything that lasts while its parts wear away must renew them at least as fast as they are lost. *Glossary:* Continuation principle.
- **renewal / renewed** — replacing what is lost, by any means (a flame is renewed by its supply). The general word; regeneration, reproduction and heredity are life's forms of it. *Avoid:* using "regeneration" for non-living renewal.
- **persist / propagate / evolvable** — the three levels at which a pattern can last: outlasting its parts, outlasting the things that carry it, and having its variations outlast their carriers. *Glossary:* Level of continuation; Propagation; Evolvability.
- **evolvable / evolvability** — carrying variants forward so selection can act on them, by any means, living or not (prions, viruses). *Glossary:* Evolvability. Heredity is evolvability achieved through a living system's own processes.
- **level** — one of the three levels above. *Avoid:* tier, stage, layer.
- **continuation condition / X continues only while** — a condition that must hold for a capability to keep going, whether the surroundings or an addition meets it (the rock pore, later a membrane, keeping the parts together). Use the noun or the verb phrase "regeneration continues only while..." consistently. *Glossary:* Continuation condition. *Avoid:* need/needs as the term, requirement, requires, prerequisite ("depends on" is reserved for the chain), support condition (retired).
- **evolutionary rescue** — a lineage surviving a change in its surroundings by carrying forward new variations that suit them, when no reachable site keeps the old conditions. *Glossary:* none yet (environment view, *Evolutionary rescue*). *Avoid:* adaptation as a synonym (adaptation need not save a lineage).

## System and parts

- **organization** — the pattern of production: which processes make which parts. *Glossary:* Organization. *Avoid:* structure, design, form, plan.
- **means** — the working parts that keep a system's processes going without being used up by them, like a catalyst or enzyme; contrasted with food, which is used up. *Glossary:* the "means" in Organizational regeneration. *Avoid:* tools, machinery (except for the cell's machinery), resources.
- **parts** — the components of a system. In prose, prefer "parts"; "components" is the glossary term.
- **processes** — the reactions or activities that make the parts. *Glossary:* Process.
- **surroundings** — everything outside the system that it does not make; may supply raw materials, energy, even a container (the rock pore). *Glossary:* Environment. Prefer "surroundings" when drawing the system/non-system line; "environment" is fine in the selection sense ("fit to its environment").
- **raw materials / food / supply** — what the system consumes. *Avoid:* calling these means.
- **mainly by its own production** — the test for where one living system ends and another begins. *Glossary:* Organizational separation (use the formal term only with a gloss).
- **functionally separate** — two networks running mostly on their own, little exchange compared with what each makes for itself. *Glossary:* Organizational separation.

## Contrast terms (near-misses)

- **accretion** — stacking material that arrives ready-made (a salt crystal); not rebuilding. *Glossary:* in Organizational regeneration.
- **pressed onto material** — how a crystal's flaw or a prion's shape is carried forward: imposed on material the system did not make. Contrast with heredity, where each new system rebuilds the variation itself.
- **copied by another system's processes** — a virus; contrast with rebuilt by its own.
- **suspended / held in stasis** — a frozen embryo: organization intact, rebuilding paused. *Glossary:* Suspended regeneration.

## Relations

- **depends on / chain** — B depends on A when B cannot occur without A, but A can occur without B. "Chain" for a sequence of such dependencies. *Glossary:* Dependency; dependency chain. *Avoid:* hierarchy for the chain itself (keep "hierarchy" for the study's title), builds on (acceptable informally, but "depends on" for the claim).
- **addition** — something beyond a capability, such as a membrane ("an addition rather than a requirement"). *Glossary:* Elaboration (synonym: addition). See open choices.

## Running examples

A/B molecules in a rock pore (and the pore field linked by channels); candle flame; salt crystal and seed chip; virus; prion; cat; mule; frozen embryo; biosphere; ship of Theseus; siphonophore; aspen grove; mitochondria. Reuse these before introducing new ones.

## Open choices (to settle)

- **variant vs. variation** — both appear in "The Chain of Life" ("particular variations" for heredity; "variants" for prions and crystals). Pick one, or fix a distinction (e.g., *variant* = the thing carried, *variation* = differences among them).
- **addition vs. elaboration** — which to use in articles when elaborations come up.
- **regeneration vs. organizational regeneration** — whether the short form is the default after first mention in every article.
