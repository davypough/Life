# Basis

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This file sets out the conceptual basis of the study: the principle any persisting pattern must satisfy, the base capability by which a living system satisfies it, the levels at which continuation occurs, and the logic by which it is elaborated. The method for assessing these proposals is in Method; definitions are in the Glossary.

## The continuation principle

A pattern whose components are lost continues only while renewal keeps pace with loss. Its components decay, disperse, are damaged, or are impaired by accumulating by-products. Unless they are replaced at least as fast as they are lost, the pattern runs down.

This is general physics, not a criterion for life. A flame satisfies it: fuel and oxygen keep arriving, and the reaction persists while they do. The principle states what any persisting process must achieve, but not how the renewal is performed.

Loss is unavoidable for any physical pattern: components have finite lifetimes, and disruption can remove many at once. A structure without renewal persists only by the durability of its parts, as a rock does, until it too wears away.

## Organizational regeneration as the proposed criterion for life

The study proposes that a living system satisfies the continuation principle in a particular way: by renewal through its own processes, which produce the components those processes depend on. In short, it **rebuilds the means of its own continuation**. This base capability is **organizational regeneration**. What persists is the network's organization, even as its individual components are replaced.

Regeneration is therefore a base capability, not the principle. The distinction separates life from other cases of persistence, enlargement, or copying:

- **Flame:** satisfies the principle, but its renewal is supplied from outside. It makes more of the radicals and heat that carry its reaction, but these are used up by the steps they drive; it produces no component that enables its steps while persisting through them, so its pattern is renewed by the fuel and oxygen that arrive.
- **Crystal:** enlarges by incorporating material into its lattice, but produces nothing: what it incorporates arrives already as its components, so even a damaged face regrows by accretion, not by renewal through its own production.
- **Virus:** is copied, but the renewal occurs through the host cell's processes, not its own.

These contrasts motivate the proposed criterion. What sufficiency means for it, and the cases that test it, are in *Sufficiency of the criterion*, below.

The criterion concerns the system's own productive contribution under suitable conditions. It does not require the system to meet every support condition for its continuation. Containment, accessible materials, usable energy, and tolerable exposure to damage may all be met by the environment. In the minimal realization of regeneration, they are (see the environment view, *Organizational regeneration*).

The detailed process account and examples are in Base Capabilities.

### Suspended regeneration

A system's regeneration is suspended to the degree that its organization persists by the durability of its parts rather than by renewal. The threshold is relative, as for organizational separation (Glossary, *Organizational separation*): a system regenerates mainly from its own production while renewal, by replacement or repair, makes good more than half of what it loses, that is, while renewal is faster than its net decline. The comparison uses the system's current rates, so a system that renews slowly but keeps pace is regenerating however slow it is. Most systems lie far from the threshold, either keeping pace or with renewal near zero; near it, chiefly while entering or leaving suspension or while running down, whether regeneration is suspended is indeterminate.

A dormant system, such as a dried tardigrade or a bacterial spore, is suspended: its organization persists, as a rock does, by the durability of its parts. Its durability differs from a rock's in two ways: the system produced it, and it preserves an organization that renews itself again. A system suspended without such a produced means, as when a network's pore dries briefly, is interrupted rather than dormant, and survives only as long as its unpreserved components last. The criterion concerns what a system does under suitable conditions, so a suspended system, dormant or not, counts as living while its preserved organization would resume regenerating when they return; a dead system's would not. Being living therefore does not turn on the threshold, and no indeterminacy passes from it down the dependency chain. Whether conditions return before the organization is lost is a support condition of regeneration, not part of the criterion; the return itself is the environment's share of it (see the environment view, *Organizational regeneration* and *Conditions only the environment can meet*): a dormant system whose conditions never return is living until its organization is lost, but does not continue. Near the end of its preserved lifetime, whether it would resume may be indeterminate. The account of dormancy is in Elaborations, *Dormancy*.

### Graded and nested living systems

Being a living system comes in degrees, because organizational separation does: a network counts as one living system to the degree that it regenerates its organization mainly from its own production (Glossary, *Organizational separation*). The degree concerns how determinately the network is counted as one system, rather than as several or as part of another; it is not a degree of integration or of individuality. Near the threshold, how many living systems there are is indeterminate; this concerns where one living system ends and another begins, not whether regeneration occurs.

Living systems can also be nested. Separation is assessed for a stated organization, so cells that each regenerate their own metabolism can together form a collective (Glossary) that regenerates a shared organization, as a biofilm renews the matrix that holds its cells and keeps nutrients near them. Such a collective is a living system for that organization, to the degree it is separated from other collectives and from its surroundings. A collective organization exists only where shared products return something to the members' regeneration, raising renewal or reducing loss; cells that export by-products into shared surroundings form none unless something returns. Cross-feeding qualifies, where one member's by-products feed another whose products return to the first (environment view, *Observations*). Being alive at this level asks little, as for any system that regenerates without reproducing. What distinguishes a collective individual, as in the major transitions, is reproduction and heredity at the collective level.

Collective heredity is heredity's support conditions applied to collectives (environment view, *Heredity*): members pass mainly from a parent collective to its offspring rather than being recruited from other collectives or the surroundings, and the benefits of shared means stay with the collective that produces them. A collective founded from one member, as a Volvox colony from one germ cell or an ant colony from one queen, meets the first condition by descent; one founded jointly by members of several lineages, as a lichen's soredia carry fungus and alga together, meets it by joint transmission. A biofilm that sheds single cells carries collective variants only weakly; a life cycle founding new mats from small groups strengthens it.[^rainey2010] Collectives assembled anew from the surroundings, as the bobtail squid acquires Vibrio in each generation, as Dictyostelium aggregates from separate cells, or as a lake's community assembles from many sources, regenerate without collective heredity. Members that take shared benefits without contributing, such as cheating cells in a Dictyostelium slug, fail the second condition.[^strassmann2000] Recursion and branching therefore differ only at collective heredity, which needs no new principle and adds no level (see *Levels and organizations*, below).

Symbiosis is nesting whose members belong to different lineages. Partners form a collective organization where each one's products return something to the other's regeneration, as when a coral receives sugars from its algae and the algae receive nitrogen and carbon dioxide; for each partner, the other's contribution is met by another system, as part of its environment. Partners are bound into one lineage only when transmitted together, as Buchnera passes through the aphid egg; partners acquired anew from the surroundings, as by the squid and by most corals, keep separate lineages, so the collective regenerates without collective heredity. A partner whose products return nothing to the other is a parasite, against which environmental protection acts. Symbiosis is therefore one route by which branching becomes recursion.

Integration (Glossary) is a separate gradient, describing the route toward fusion: how far members' regeneration depends on support conditions met only by their collective. Buchnera is separate, since its own genes make nearly all its proteins, but wholly dependent on its host. Joint transmission lets dependence deepen into gene loss and then, where the host comes to make the member's proteins, into loss of separation. The Paulinella chromatophore, which imports hundreds of proteins made from nuclear genes,[^singer2017] and insect symbionts with the smallest genomes[^mccutcheon2012] lie near the threshold. A mitochondrion, most of whose proteins are made from nuclear genes and imported, no longer regenerates mainly from its own production, so it has become a component rather than a nested living system.[^keeling2008] Cells that no longer renew their own proteins, such as mammalian erythrocytes, phloem sieve elements kept by their companion cells, and lens fibre cells, are likewise components of their body rather than nested living systems. Endosymbiosis thus passes through the graded zone in the reverse direction from fission. Integration is not a condition of being a collective: dependence also grows where none forms, as Prochlorococcus depends on neighboring bacteria to remove hydrogen peroxide from open seawater, which separates no collective of them from the rest.

The criterion extends beyond familiar collectives. A lake that recycles its nutrients mainly within itself, and the biosphere, whose cycles return materials to producers many times over, pass both the test of a shared organization and the test of separation; the biosphere is maximally separated, since there is no other biosphere to exchange with. Each is a living system for its organization, though a thin one: neither reproduces nor carries heredity, so neither is a collective individual.[^lovelock1974][^doolittle2019] A city, whose means are renewed mainly by production elsewhere, lies near or below the threshold. These are accepted consequences rather than reasons for a further condition. Requiring integration would exclude the biofilm while admitting the biosphere, on which every heterotroph depends; requiring a jointly produced means, rather than exchanged materials, would still admit the biosphere with its ozone layer; and requiring collective reproduction would make being alive depend on reproduction, against the dependency view. Being a living system and being an individual come apart, as metabolic wholes and lineages do.[^dupre2009]

A virus is never a nested living system, since it does not regenerate: while expressed in a cell it is a component of the cell's network, diverting its production, and as a virion it is environment. An endogenous retrovirus, transmitted with its host's genome, has become a host component, in some cases one that provides a function, as syncytins do in the placenta; joint transmission absorbed its lineage without its ever having been a living system. A transmissible cancer moves the other way: a somatic cell, a nested living system without a lineage beyond its body, founds an independent lineage passed between hosts, a parasite against which the hosts' environmental protection acts.[^murchison2014]

### Counting and continuity

Separation counts living systems at one time; identity over time is continuity of organization (Glossary). Fission and fusion change the count without breaking continuity: an aspen clone is one living system while its roots connect its stems and several once they die back, while its organization continues throughout. A living system can therefore end in three ways: by death, when its organization would no longer resume; by absorption, when its regeneration continues but is no longer mainly its own, as with the mitochondrion's ancestor; or by symmetric fission, where the lineage continues and whether the parent survives as either offspring needs no answer. Collectives and members end independently. Members die while a colony or biofilm continues, and a Dictyostelium slug's collective organization ends at fruiting, with its stalk cells dead and its spores dormant one by one, while members continue. A collective counts as dormant only while its own organization would resume: a dried lichen resumes as a lichen, whereas Dictyostelium spores resume as separate cells.

### Sufficiency of the criterion

The criterion is not meant to match ordinary usage of "living", and cannot: the minimal realization of regeneration, the A/B network in a mineral pore, which is the starting environment for every elaboration of the living system (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*), is not alive in ordinary usage. The criterion is a stipulation that marks a distinct way of persisting, renewal of a system's produced means by its own production, as against renewal by supply (the flame), persistence by durability (the rock), and copying by another system's processes (the virus).[^cleland2012] It is sufficient in this sense where two things hold. First, no system determinately meets the criterion while persisting in one of those other ways; such a case calls for restating a definition, as a counterexample to a definitional dependency does (Method, *Testing relations against cases*). Second, the systems it admits beyond ordinary usage are listed as accepted consequences, each because any condition excluding it would also exclude a starting environment or make being alive depend on reproduction, against the dependency view. The test a candidate must pass is in Method, *Testing the criterion for life*. The result holds for the candidates assessed below, as completeness does for the elaborations (Method, *Completeness of the elaborations*).

The means are the decisive point. A branching-chain flame makes more of the radicals its reaction depends on, so it would meet a criterion requiring only that a system produce components its processes depend on. What it lacks is a produced component that enables a production step without being transformed by it, as a catalyst, template, or boundary does; its radicals and heat are used up by the steps they drive (Glossary, *Organizational regeneration*). The line follows the distinction between catalysis and autocatalysis by consumption in the literature on autocatalytic sets,[^hordijk] and resembles the requirement that constraints be conserved at the time scale of the processes they act on.[^montevil2015] Regeneration is therefore narrower than autocatalysis in the broad sense, in which a network counts as autocatalytic if it makes more of its own members by any route: the formose reaction is autocatalytic in that sense but does not regenerate. It is closest to a RAF set whose renewal keeps pace with loss, though its means include templates and boundaries as well as catalysts.

**Candidates that do not meet the criterion.**

- **Autocatalysis by consumption:** the flame; the formose reaction, in which glycolaldehyde is consumed and doubled in each cycle while the base that catalyzes it is supplied;[^breslow1959] reaction-diffusion patterns driven by such autocatalysis or by a supplied catalyst, as in the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction; and a hurricane, a flow pattern whose water vapour and heat are used up as they drive it, with no produced component. Each makes more of what its steps transform, a pattern renewed by its supply (definitional; unconditional).
- **Accretion:** a crystal, as above, and structures that assemble from components supplied as such (definitional; unconditional).
- **Renewal by another system's processes:** viruses (*Graded and nested living systems*, above), and programs, such as digital organisms and computer viruses, copied by hardware they do not produce. Simulated chemistries are models of living systems, outside the study's scope (Method, *Approach*) (definitional; unconditional).
- **Renewal mainly by production elsewhere:** a city or a firm, whose means are renewed mainly by what other systems produce, lies near or below the threshold of separation (definitional; conditional on that balance).

**Accepted consequences.** Each is a thin living system for its organization: it regenerates, and reproduction or heredity, where present, are further base capabilities.

- **Catalytic networks in vitro:** Lincoln and Joyce's cross-catalytic ribozymes, fed substrates, regenerate, and with serial transfer they reproduce and carry heredity (realization view, *A primitive A/B network example*); a realized RAF set regenerates where its renewal makes good most of its losses and it is separated; and so does a reaction-diffusion spot of a catalytic network (Base Capabilities, *Reproduction*). Each is definitional in ground and conditional on renewal predominating (Method, *Testing the criterion for life*); when supply stops, the system is suspended and living while it would resume.
- **One-process systems:** a template that copies itself regenerates where each template acts more than once before it is lost; where products stay bound to templates, so that growth is parabolic, it lies near the threshold. Micelles or vesicles that make their own amphiphile from a supplied precursor[^bachmann1992] propagate without regenerating in a closed vessel, where nothing lost is renewed, but would regenerate in a flow with dilution, the whole population being one system, since its members exchange components fast (definitional; conditional). Requiring two or more processes would be a minimal complexity without a natural crossover.
- **Collectives with human members:** the global economy, whose means are renewed mainly by its own production and which returns food and other means to its members' regeneration, is a thin living system nested in the biosphere, as the lake and the biosphere are (*Graded and nested living systems*, above); national economies lie near the threshold by their share of trade (definitional; conditional).
- **Made systems:** a hypothetical factory that makes and repairs its machines from raw materials would regenerate. The criterion does not depend on the chemistry, as the thresholds do not (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*) (definitional; conditional).

**Further conditions proposed in the literature.** Each is definitional in ground and unconditional in scope, and each is rejected for its cost, as integration, a jointly produced means, and collective reproduction are (*Graded and nested living systems*, above).

- **A produced boundary,** as in autopoiesis,[^varela1974] Gánti's chemoton,[^ganti2003] Deacon's autogen,[^deacon2011] and the autonomy of Ruiz-Mirazo, Peretó, and Moreno:[^ruizmirazo2004] it would make individuation a dependency of regeneration, excluding the pore, the pore field, spots, and Lincoln and Joyce's ribozymes, and contradicting the environment view's entry for containment.
- **Energy transduction,** also in their account: it would exclude regeneration on energy-rich R and change the starting environment (Elaborations, *Energy transduction*).
- **A template or heredity,** as in Gánti's information-carrying subsystem and their "records": being alive would depend on heredity, and so on reproduction, against the dependency view (the mule and the sterile worker).
- **Darwinian evolution,** as in the NASA working definition, a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution:[^joyce1994] the same cost, with an individual's status depending on its lineage. In the study's terms it describes an evolving lineage of living systems, its "self-sustaining" part being regeneration.
- **Autonomy or agency:** Ruiz-Mirazo, Peretó, and Moreno join a boundary, transduction, and records with open-ended evolution, an outcome graded over an interval (Method, *Dependency criterion*), so being alive would depend on the interval chosen; agency, adjusting activity to the surroundings, is environmental responsiveness, an elaboration.
- **A minimal complexity:** there is no natural crossover, so it would be a convention, and it would exclude minimal realizations.
- **Gánti's real and potential criteria:** his real criteria of inherent unity, metabolism, and inherent stability correspond to separation, regeneration, and renewal keeping pace with loss; his potential criteria of growth and reproduction, hereditary change and evolution, and mortality correspond to the lineage, the evolving lineage, and death (*Counting and continuity*, above). The study departs from him only at his real criteria of an information-carrying subsystem, rejected as for a template, and program control, which would make self-regulation, an elaboration, a condition.

**Necessity.** No living system assessed lacks regeneration. Dormant systems are suspended (*Suspended regeneration*, above); viruses and prions do not regenerate and are not living systems (*Graded and nested living systems*, above, and *Three levels of continuation for living systems*, below); and cells that no longer renew their own proteins are components of their body (*Graded and nested living systems*, above).

## Three levels of continuation for living systems

The continuation principle applies at more than one level. At each level something continues, renewal occurs through a base capability, and characteristic losses must be made good.

| Level | What continues | Base capability | Lost through |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living system | Its organization | Regeneration | Decay, dispersal, damage, by-product build-up |
| Lineage | The organization across systems | Reproduction | Death or dissolution of systems |
| Evolving lineage | Particular variants | Heredity | Transmission error, mixing, failure of carrier systems |

At each level, continuation can be described in general terms that apply to any process: a system persists, a succession of systems propagates, and a succession that carries forward particular variants is evolvable. A flame persists without regenerating, a crystal or virus propagates without reproducing, and a crystal can carry its defects into new crystals without an organization of its own to transmit. Components that move between living systems can also propagate and carry variants: viruses, plasmids, and transposons; organelles such as mitochondria; and prions, whose strains are copied and selected in cell culture without regenerating.[^li2010] Their evolvability is that of components, not heredity, which transmits a living system's organization; near the threshold of separation, as for endosymbionts becoming organelles, whether their transmission is heredity is indeterminate (see *Graded and nested living systems*, above). Regeneration, reproduction, and heredity are the forms of persistence, propagation, and evolvability that occur through a living system's own processes.

Each base capability presupposes the one before it; the dependency view justifies these relations and their direction.

Each level poses its own question for the study:

- **Living system:** what enables regeneration to continue under particular conditions?
- **Lineage:** what enables living systems to originate new living systems and sustain a lineage?
- **Evolving lineage:** what enables a lineage to carry forward particular variants on which selection can act?

Open-ended evolution is a possible extended endpoint of the analysis. It is an outcome that base capabilities and elaborations may make possible, not a base capability or elaboration itself.

### Levels and organizations

The three levels are forms of continuation, not sizes or organizations: a pattern persists, a succession of patterns propagates, and a succession carries forward particular variants. Each is assessed for a stated organization, member or collective, as organizational separation is (Glossary, *Organizational separation*). In the literature on major transitions and levels of selection, a "level" is an organization, such as a gene, cell, or colony;[^maynardsmith1995][^okasha2006] in this study it is a form of continuation.

A further level would need a further form of continuation: something that continues that is not, for any stated organization, an organization, a succession of organizations, or particular variants carried by a succession, with losses not among those in the table above, and a base capability, renewing it through living systems' own processes, that is not regeneration, reproduction, or heredity of some stated organization. A new continuant of an existing form, such as a collective individual, applies the three levels to another organization and is not a further level. The criterion is definitional and unconditional. It is stated so that a case could meet it; the result holds for the directions assessed below, as completeness does for the elaborations (Method, *Completeness of the elaborations*).

- **Recursion.** A collective individual is the dependency chain assessed for a collective's organization: it regenerates shared means, reproduces as a collective, and carries collective variants, in that order (*Graded and nested living systems*, above). The major transitions add integration, a descriptive gradient; division of labor, a mechanism; policing, existing elaborations applied to the collective (Elaborations, *Candidates assessed*); and, at the limit, loss of members' separation, which ends them as nested living systems by absorption (*Counting and continuity*, above). None is a base capability. Godfrey-Smith's graded criteria for collective Darwinian individuals correspond: a bottleneck is founding by descent from one member, a germ line is division of labor, and his integration is the integration gradient (Glossary, *Integration*).[^godfreysmith2009] Members' heredity can be a mechanism of collective heredity, as a Volvox germ cell's genome carries the colony's variants, and so can member composition, as the proportion of partners in a lichen's soredia; this is a relation between realizations (realization view, *Observations*), not a dependency between levels. Recursion is definitional in ground and unconditional in scope.
- **Branching.** A community of lineages that renew one another's support conditions, each as part of the others' environment, continues only at the level of the living system: it is a thin living system where it passes the tests of a shared organization and of separation, as a lake and the biosphere do, and otherwise none (*Graded and nested living systems*, above). Without collective reproduction it has no lineage of its own, and so no heredity; changes in its composition are its member lineages' evolvability. A community that reproduces by joint transmission has become recursion. Selection on collectives that changes only members' fitness, without collective offspring, is one of the two forms of multilevel selection the literature distinguishes; the other, counted in collective offspring, is recursion.[^okasha2006] Branching therefore identifies no further continuant (definitional; unconditional as to level, with whether a given community is a living system assessed for it).
- **Culture.** A learned state is a lasting state of the tracking means of environmental responsiveness (Elaborations, *Candidates assessed*, learning and memory). It passes to another living system through the learner's own responsiveness, not in reproduction; a parent who teaches is part of its offspring's environment, as modified surroundings are (environment view, *Observations*). For organisms, cultural variants are therefore the evolvability of components, states moving between living systems as prion strains do, not heredity; this departs by definition from accounts treating culture as a second inheritance system of individuals.[^boyd1985] For a collective, such as a society, culture is a means of inheritance of collective heredity where the collective reproduces, as by fission of a band or founding of a colony; where founders carry it, so that transmission from parent to offspring collectives outweighs borrowing between collectives; and where benefits stay with the collective that produces them (environment view, *Heredity*). Where cultural variants pass freely between collectives, collectives carry no heredity and evolvability attaches to the variants, as in a pore field whose heritable components move freely (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*); near the threshold it is indeterminate. Modular culture, such as sequences of symbols, has the capacity of modular inheritance (Glossary, *Heredity*) but falls on the same side of this line as any other culture; as a new means of inheritance it does not divide heredity (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).[^szathmary2015] Culture is definitional in ground and conditional in scope, on collective reproduction and the balance of vertical and horizontal transmission between collectives.
- **Directed variation.** A means raising variation takes over nothing, and one setting a variant by a tracked condition and carrying it into new units is transgenerational induction; deliberate innovation in culture is learning (Elaborations, *Candidates assessed*). It is net beneficial to open-ended evolution, never necessary (definitional; unconditional).

None of these changes the dependency chain or the base capabilities. Collective heredity is net beneficial to open-ended evolution as a source of new collective individuals (Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*).

## Elaborations as additions

**Potential elaborations** of regeneration are individuation, resource provisioning, energy transduction, self-regulation, environmental responsiveness, environmental protection, waste management, heat gain, and dormancy. They are produced by a regenerating network, but no base capability in the dependency chain depends on them. Whether a system needs one depends on which support conditions its environment meets, so they need not be possessed by every living system. Unlike a base capability, an elaboration does not define a level.

### The balance between renewal and loss

Each elaboration acts on the balance between renewal and loss:

- **Raise renewal:** resource provisioning, energy transduction, heat gain (which, against freezing, also reduces loss).
- **Reduce loss:** individuation, environmental protection, waste management, dormancy (slowing loss while renewal is suspended).
- **Change activity with conditions:** self-regulation, on the system's own levels, keeping component proportions workable; environmental responsiveness, on outside conditions. Both also time production and the other elaborations (environment view, *Observations*).

### Logic of elaboration

1. Regeneration depends on support conditions, which the environment may meet.
2. Environments fall short, and the scarcest support condition limits persistence.
3. A productive network can produce additional components.
4. An addition persists only if it is itself regenerated and is net beneficial to the balance at its level. For an elaboration of reproduction, that is the lineage's balance between originating systems and losing them, even at a cost to the parent, as in provisioning offspring; for an elaboration of heredity, it is the balance between carrying particular variants forward and losing them.

When an addition meets a support condition that the environment previously met, the condition is **taken over** by the new elaboration. The environment view records which conditions can be taken over, by which elaborations, and which parts only the environment can meet.

Net benefit is weaker than necessity. An elaboration is necessary for continuation (Glossary) where, without it, the environment's share of a support condition is less than the base capability can make good; it is net beneficial wherever it improves the balance after its own cost. Necessity implies net benefit, but not the reverse. Along any one condition, an elaboration therefore passes from a net cost, where the environment meets the condition with margin, through net benefit without necessity, to necessity, and finally to insufficiency, where the shortfall exceeds what it can make good or lies in the part only the environment can meet. Necessity is always relative to a stated system and environment: an elaboration meets a condition the environment might otherwise meet (Glossary, *Elaboration*), so none is necessary across the stated range of realizations, which is why none enters the dependency chain. Where several elaborations meet one condition, necessity attaches only to the share one of them takes over alone, and a combination is necessary where no part alone meets the shortfall. Near the threshold necessity is graded, and since elaborations are not in the dependency chain, nothing passes from it to the chain. The thresholds for each elaboration are stated in its account (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*).

Additions arise from regeneration itself: a productive network can make components that occur nowhere in its surroundings, through side reactions and, once heredity occurs, through transmission errors and recombination. Being new adds no capability by itself. An addition contributes only by meeting a support condition or by changing what one requires (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*); one that does neither adds nothing to the balance. What matters for elaboration is that an addition is new to the system or lineage; whether it also occurs in the surroundings is incidental.

Before reproduction and heredity, elaborations arise only as single chance retentions: a system may happen to produce and keep an addition, but the addition ends with that system. Cumulative elaboration, one addition building on another, needs reproduction to carry additions into new systems and heredity to carry particular additions forward. The process is undirected: regeneration generates additions, reproduction and heredity carry them forward, and those that persist are the ones net beneficial at their level (step 4).
