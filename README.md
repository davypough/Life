# A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes

What is life? This study proposes one answer: a living system **rebuilds the means of its own continuation**. Its processes produce and renew the working parts that keep those processes going. The study calls this **organizational regeneration** and treats it as a proposed criterion for life.

From that starting point, the study asks how the basic life process is elaborated under different environmental conditions, and builds a functional hierarchy of the major capabilities of life. The hierarchy has three levels: the living system (regeneration), the lineage (reproduction), and the evolving lineage (heredity). It is a conceptual study, meant to complement what the historical record of life on Earth shows.

The study was prompted by Wolfgang Stegemann's *A Primer for Philosophers of Mind: How to get from Life to Consciousness* (2026).

## Where to start

Begin with **overview.md** for a short summary of the study and its main proposals. Then read:

1. **basis.md**: the continuation principle, regeneration as the proposed criterion for life, and the three levels of continuation.
2. **method.md**: the approach, environmental assumptions, and how the proposals are tested.
3. **dependencies.md**, **environment.md**, and **realization.md**: three views of how capabilities depend on one another, what they need from their surroundings, and the mechanisms that provide them.
4. **capabilities.md** and **elaborations.md**: reference accounts of each base capability and each elaboration.
5. **lifes-future.md**: a speculative companion applying the framework to the future of life.
6. **luca.md**: a supplement relating the study to current research on the last universal common ancestor (LUCA).

**glossary.md** holds all definitions, and **reading-list.md** lists the sources.
