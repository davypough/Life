# Life's Future: A Speculative Analysis

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This file applies the study's framework to the future of life. It is speculative: the main analysis is a conceptual study of what life depends on, not a forecast, and nothing here enters the hierarchy or its views. Each pattern below rests on a claim with its home in the main analysis, cited in plain text; what is added here is only what follows from those claims when they are projected forward. Like the hierarchy, the patterns constrain what can happen without determining what will (Method, *Scope and limitations*).

## Patterns the framework sets

### Few new elaborations, many new mechanisms

An elaboration is missing only where a produced means takes over a contribution that no current elaboration takes over (Method, *Completeness of the elaborations*). Almost every contribution the surroundings make, as listed in the environment view's "Met by the environment" entries, is already taken over by some elaboration, and the parts only the environment can meet cannot be taken over at all (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*). Future life is therefore likely to add new mechanisms, specialized applications, and combinations far more often than new elaborations. Elaborations are few because they are identified by contribution, whereas novelty is counted in mechanisms (Glossary, *Open-ended evolution*). A new elaboration would most likely appear only where a support condition itself is newly met by the surroundings in some new environment and then taken over, or where a condition the study has not identified comes to limit continuation.

### Dependence shifts but never ends

Taking over a condition shifts dependence onto the elaboration's own support conditions, and ultimately onto the parts only the environment can meet: raw materials and an energy source, a medium and physical range the system's means can work within, the return of suitable conditions after an interruption, and disruptions that spare some of a lineage's sites or times (environment view, *Observations* and *Conditions only the environment can meet*). However elaborated, future living systems remain bounded by these. Elaboration widens the range of environments life can occupy without making any environment suitable, as heat gain and protection do (Elaborations, *Heat gain* and *Environmental protection*).

### Spreading is the only answer to the largest disruptions

At the lineage level, the environment's share is that disruptions spare some of the sites or times a lineage occupies. Dispersal and bet-hedging spread a lineage over more sites and times but cannot make the surroundings spare any (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*). All of Earth's lineages, and the biosphere as a thin living system (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*), share one site at the largest scale, so their losses are correlated there. Changes in the energy source or the planet that end liquid water would be such a disruption. The framework implies that only spreading to other sites could meet that condition for any of them; dormancy lengthens an interruption a system survives but cannot bring conditions back (Elaborations, *Dormancy*).

### New individuals by recursion, not new levels

There is no level beyond the evolving lineage; new continuants arise by applying the three levels to a further organization (Basis, *Levels and organizations*). Future major transitions would therefore be new collective individuals: collectives that regenerate a shared organization, reproduce as collectives, and carry collective heredity, typically by joint transmission of members followed by deepening integration (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*). Symbiosis is the route by which branching becomes recursion, so new collective individuals are most likely to arise from partners already transmitted together.

### Human societies and the global economy

The global economy is a thin living system nested in the biosphere (Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*, accepted consequences). It is not a collective individual: it neither reproduces nor carries heredity, and as the only one of its kind it has no offspring collectives. Culture is collective heredity only where collectives reproduce, founders carry it, and transmission from parent to offspring collectives outweighs borrowing between them (Basis, *Levels and organizations*). Where cultural variants pass freely between societies, as they increasingly do, societies carry no heredity of their own and evolvability attaches to the variants. The framework implies that human collectives would become collective individuals only by founding separated offspring collectives, such as settlements at other sites, that carry their culture mainly by descent. The same step would also spread the lineages involved across sites.

### Made systems

The criterion does not depend on chemistry: a factory that makes and repairs its machines from raw materials would regenerate (Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*, made systems). Made systems would follow the same dependency chain. Such a system would be a lineage only if it originated new systems that regenerate mainly from their own production, and an evolving lineage only if those systems resembled their parent more than others, with modular inheritance offering the most heritable variants (Glossary, *Heredity*). Programs copied by hardware they do not produce remain models, as digital organisms are (Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*). Made systems that regenerate their own hardware would be thin living systems first, and evolving lineages only by the further base capabilities.

### Open-ended evolution needs a changing world

Open-ended evolution needs new conditions or environments to keep becoming available; within a fixed set, successive improvements give diminishing returns (Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*, the environment's share). Other lineages supply new conditions only as far as their own mechanisms are new. The framework therefore implies that life's continued novelty depends on change it cannot wholly supply for itself: new environments entered, change in the surroundings, or new mechanisms in other lineages, including human technology and made systems, to the extent that they are new rather than recurring.

## What the framework does not say

- It sets no timeline and predicts no particular sequence; functional dependency constrains possible sequences without establishing an actual one (Method, *Dependency criterion*).
- It implies no progress: greater elaboration is not better performance in every environment (Method, *Scope and limitations*).
- It does not say whether any of these possibilities will be realized, only what each would depend on.
