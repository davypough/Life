# Glossary

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This file is the primary home for the study's definitions. Other files use these terms without redefining them. The arguments for the proposals are in Basis, Method, and the views; the accounts are in Base Capabilities and Elaborations.

## General terms

- **Environment:** the surroundings of a system, including conditions and resources that support or constrain its activity. The distinction is drawn by production, not location: means the system produces and renews belong to the system; conditions it relies on but does not produce or renew are environmental, even when physically adjacent, such as a containing mineral pore. A produced means outside the system's other components, such as a secreted matrix or siderophores, belongs to the system while it is a working component, renewed or retained by it. A product released and not renewed, such as a mineral deposit outlasting its maker, is environmental, even for its producer and the producer's offspring. A means made jointly by several systems belongs to the collective whose organization includes it (realization view, *Observations*).
- **By-product:** a product of a system's activity that is not one of its working components, such as residue in the A/B network, reactive oxygen from respiration, or heat released by its activity. It is formed by processes the system's network produces, including side reactions, even where they use outside materials or energy, as when the cell's own enzymes react accidentally with oxygen or its photosynthesis makes singlet oxygen. It stays the system's by-product wherever it goes, including after export. An agent formed when an outside influence acts directly on the system's components, as when UV striking its molecules forms hydrogen peroxide, is part of that influence. For a given system, by-products of other systems, like anything else arriving from the surroundings that the system did not produce, are outside influences. Like organizational separation, this is assessed for a stated organization: other members' by-products are outside influences for a member and by-products of their collective, where the members form one (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*). This distinction separates waste management from environmental protection (see Elaborations, *Environmental protection*).
- **Collective:** a living system some of whose components are themselves living systems (members), each regenerating its own organization. It is a living system for the organization the members regenerate together, such as a biofilm's cells and matrix (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*).
- **Collective individual:** a collective that reproduces as a collective and carries collective heredity, so that the dependency chain holds for its shared organization. Being a collective asks only regeneration of that organization, so a lake or the biosphere is a collective but not a collective individual (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems* and *Levels and organizations*).
- **Continuity of organization:** the identity of a living system over time: the same organization persisting without being lost, regenerated while its components are replaced or preserved while regeneration is suspended (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*). Separation counts systems at one time; continuity follows a system through fission and fusion (Basis, *Counting and continuity*).
- **Energy carrier:** a component or resource whose reaction releases energy that drives a process of the system, such as energy-rich R, activated R, or ATP. A carrier may arrive from the surroundings or be charged by energy transduction.
- **Growth:** an increase in a system's components because its production exceeds its losses. It is organizational regeneration producing beyond replacement, not a separate base capability or elaboration. Enlargement by accretion, as when a crystal incorporates material into its lattice, is not growth in this sense. On its role in reproduction, see Base Capabilities, *Reproduction*.
- **Integration:** the degree to which members' regeneration depends on support conditions met only by their collective. It is distinct from organizational separation: a member can be separate and wholly dependent, as Buchnera is in the aphid. At its limit, when the collective makes most of a member's components, the member is a component rather than a nested living system (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*).
- **Lineage:** a succession of living systems, each originating from an earlier one.
- **Living system:** a system whose processes regenerate its own organization under suitable conditions (the study's proposed criterion for life). A system whose regeneration is suspended, dormant or not, counts as living while its preserved organization would resume regenerating when conditions return (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*). Being a living system comes in degrees, with organizational separation, and living systems can be nested, each for its own organization (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*). Being one living system is distinct from being a collective individual, and its identity over time is continuity of organization (Basis, *Counting and continuity*). The criterion is a stipulation marking a distinct way of persisting, not a match with ordinary usage (Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*).
- **Mechanism:** organized components of a system, together with the processes they take part in, that the system's network produces and renews. A mechanism may be a single structure with its processes, such as a membrane, or a whole network, such as the A/B cycle that provides organizational regeneration.
- **Minimal realization:** a system having a given base capability or elaboration under explicitly stated environmental conditions, with no part beyond what it needs: it produces and renews only what that base capability or elaboration needs, and the environment meets every other support condition, for an elaboration as in the minimal realization of the base capability it elaborates (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*). There may be several, differing in size (realization view, *A primitive A/B network example*).
- **Network:** processes linked by what each produces and consumes. A network may be described in general, as a type (a network in which A and B produce each other), or as the network of a particular system.
- **Organization:** the pattern of production relations in a network, which persists while its components are replaced. It is what organizational regeneration renews, and what "its own" refers to.
- **Organizational separation:** the condition under which two networks are separate systems: each regenerates its organization mainly from its own production, so that gross exchange of the components that organization renews is slower than each network's own renewal. "Mainly" marks a natural crossover: when exchange is slower than renewal, more than half of each network's components were made within it. The threshold is relative to renewal, not fixed, so it applies alike to fast and slow chemistries. Separation itself comes in degrees, as biological individuality and reproduction do;[^queller2009][^clarke2010][^godfreysmith2009] near the threshold, the number of systems is indeterminate. Separation is assessed for a stated organization and, like the distinction from the environment, is drawn by production, not location. Its degree concerns how determinately networks are counted as separate systems, not how far members depend on a collective (see *Integration*). For a collective, it is assessed between members, for their own organizations, and between the collective and other collectives or its surroundings, for the shared organization.
- **Process:** a physical or chemical change. A network type specifies process types; a particular system contains process occurrences.
- **Starting environment:** the environment of a base capability's minimal realization: the single pore (regeneration), the pore field (reproduction), and the pore field with compositional inheritance (heredity). Each elaboration's minimal realization departs from it in one support condition (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*).
- **Suspended regeneration:** a system's organization persisting mainly by the durability of its parts rather than by renewal, where renewal makes good less than half of what is lost, a graded, relative threshold. It is dormant where a produced preserving means holds the organization, and interrupted otherwise; either way the system counts as living while its organization would resume (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*).
- **System:** a particular set of interacting components and their processes, actual or hypothetical, such as a flame, a crystal, the A/B network, or a cell. It is distinguished from its environment as described in *Environment*; the distinction need not be a sharp boundary and may be only a gradient, as in a reaction-diffusion spot. Living systems are told apart from one another by production relations: components belong to one system when their processes renew one another (see *Organizational separation*).

## Base capability and elaboration

- **Base capability:** the form of renewal, in living systems, that meets the continuation principle at one level: organizational regeneration (living system), reproduction (lineage), or heredity (evolving lineage). It is identified by what it renews under suitable conditions, not by the particular processes or physical means involved, so it may have different realizations. Without its base capability, nothing continues at that level.
- **Elaboration** (synonym: **addition**): a contribution beyond a base capability, in which a living system produces and renews means that meet a support condition the environment might otherwise meet.

## Continuation and levels

- **Continuation principle:** a pattern whose components are lost, gradually or suddenly, continues only while renewal keeps pace with loss. See Basis, *The continuation principle*.
- **Error threshold:** the error rate per transmission above which a lineage's particular variant is lost faster than selection can maintain it. Roughly, the heritable information in a transmitted set, counted in units where errors matter, cannot exceed about one over the error rate per unit of the copying step, whatever means of inheritance provides it; dividing the information among several copied units does not remove the limit.[^eigen1971] See Elaborations, *Error correction*.
- **Evolvability:** the capacity of a succession of systems to carry forward particular variants, by any means, so that selection can act on them. Heredity is the form it takes in living systems; components moving between living systems, such as viruses and plasmids, can be evolvable without heredity. See Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*.
- **Evolving lineage:** a lineage in which particular variants are carried forward across generations.
- **Horizontal transfer:** passage of heritable components between systems of different lineages, other than from parent to offspring. It contrasts with **vertical transmission**, from parent (or parents) to offspring in reproduction. On the balance heredity needs between them, see the environment view, *Heredity*.
- **Level of continuation (level):** one of three forms of continuation, each presupposing the one before: a system persists, a succession of systems propagates, and a succession that carries forward particular variants is evolvable. For living systems, the levels are the living system, the lineage, and the evolving lineage, each with its own continuant, base capability, and characteristic losses. Each is assessed for a stated organization, member or collective; a collective individual is these levels assessed for a collective's organization, not a further level, and a "level" in the literature on levels of selection corresponds to an organization rather than to a level here. See Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems* and *Levels and organizations*.
- **Open-ended evolution:** evolution in which, over a stated interval and environment, evolving lineages keep producing new heritable mechanisms that persist, mechanisms that provide base capabilities, elaborations, or combinations or that let lineages enter environments they did not occupy before, at a rate showing no evident decline. It is graded by that rate and interval. New heritable variants of existing mechanisms alone do not meet it, since a fixed store of heritable information yields them without limit by drift. Novelty is counted in mechanisms (realization view) rather than in elaborations, which are identified by contribution and are few.[^taylor2016][^bedau1998] The study treats it as a possible extended endpoint, an outcome rather than a base capability or elaboration; its conditions are in Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*.
- **Propagation:** continuation of a pattern across a succession of systems, by any means, as when a crystal seeds new crystals or a virus is copied. Reproduction is the form it takes in living systems. See Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*.
- **Vertical transmission:** see *Horizontal transfer*.

## Relations

- **Combination:** two or more base capabilities or elaborations operating together, one timing, placing, or distributing another's contribution, with no produced means beyond those of its parts, such as responsive protection (environmental responsiveness switching on protection) or provisioning offspring (reproduction placing stores in new units). A base capability is a part only where the other part is not one of its own elaborations. A combination is not a further elaboration; it is assessed through its parts and their relations (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).
- **Conditional dependency:** a dependency, definitional or physical, that holds only under particular environmental conditions or for particular realizations. A dependency that holds across the stated range of realizations is **unconditional**.
- **Definitional dependency:** a dependency that follows from what the base capabilities and elaborations mean.
- **Dependency:** B depends on A when B cannot occur without A while A can occur without B, within the stated scope (the dependency criterion; see Method). A is then a **prerequisite** of B. A **dependency chain** is a sequence of such relations.
- **Physical dependency:** a dependency that follows from what chemistry and physics permit, not from meaning alone.
- **Provides:** the relation between a mechanism and a base capability or elaboration that occurs through it in a particular system. A mechanism provides a base capability or elaboration when its operation is how that base capability or elaboration occurs in that system. For example, renewing the jelly is how individuation occurs in the A/B network, and renewing the membrane is how it occurs in a cell. The relation is constitutive, not causal: the mechanism does not produce the base capability or elaboration as a separate effect. Nor is it mere enabling: environmental conditions may make a base capability possible by meeting its support conditions, but only the system's own mechanisms provide it. One mechanism may provide several base capabilities or elaborations; one base capability or elaboration may be provided by different mechanisms in different systems; and several mechanisms may jointly provide one within a system. See the realization view.
- **Specialization (specialized application):** a narrower case of a broader base capability or elaboration, such as UV protection as a case of environmental protection, or unlimited heredity as a case of heredity. It falls under the broader one rather than depending on it, so it adds no step to a dependency chain.

## Environment and support

- **Necessary for continuation:** of an elaboration, for a stated system in a stated environment: the system continues with it and would not without it, because the environment's share of a support condition is less than the base capability can make good. At the living-system level, renewal would fall behind loss, or a dormant organization would be lost before conditions return; at the lineage level, complete new systems would reach sites more slowly than systems are lost; at the evolving-lineage level, particular variants would be lost faster than they are carried forward. See Basis, *Logic of elaboration*.
- **Necessary for, net beneficial to, or sufficient for an outcome:** of a condition, for an outcome graded over an interval, such as open-ended evolution, in a stated environment and over a stated interval. A condition is necessary for the outcome where the outcome fails over that interval without it, and net beneficial to it where it raises the outcome's rate without being necessary. A set of conditions is sufficient for the outcome where no case meets every condition throughout the interval while the outcome determinately fails. These relations are not assessed for each occurrence of a base capability or elaboration, as dependencies are (Method, *Dependency criterion*); sufficiency is tested by cases (Method, *Testing the conditions of an outcome*).
- **Net beneficial:** of an elaboration, improving the balance between renewal and loss at its level, after the cost of producing and renewing its means, compared with the same system without it. Necessary for continuation implies net beneficial, but not the reverse (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*).
- **Support condition:** a condition a base capability needs in order to continue, whether it is met by the environment, by an elaboration, or partly by each. Containment is the same support condition whether a mineral pore or a membrane meets it.
- **Taking over:** a support condition coming to be met by an elaboration rather than by the environment. Part of some support conditions cannot be taken over (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*).

## The named base capabilities and elaborations

### Base capabilities

- **Organizational regeneration:** renewal of a network's organization by its own processes, which produce the components those processes depend on. In short, the network rebuilds the means of its own continuation. The means are produced components that each enable more than one occurrence of a production step, on average, without being transformed by it, such as catalysts, templates, and boundaries; making more of a material the steps consume, as in a flame or the formose reaction, is not regeneration. Producing means transforms supplied materials; incorporating components that arrive as such, as a crystal does, is accretion. The study's proposed criterion for life; on its sufficiency, see Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*.
- **Reproduction:** originating new living systems from the components and processes of an existing one.
- **Heredity:** transmitting a specific organization to new living systems in reproduction, so that they resemble their parent more than other systems in the population. It comes in degrees, by how strongly they resemble their parent. On variation, see the dependency view, *Heredity*. **Inheritance** is the means by which heredity occurs, such as compositional inheritance or template copying. Inheritance is **modular** where heritable units vary independently and are copied by one rule regardless of their content, as in template copying, so that possible variants multiply with length without new chemistry.[^szathmary2000]

### Elaborations of the living system

- **Dormancy:** preserving the system's organization through an interruption of its support conditions, by produced means that slow the loss of its components while regeneration is suspended (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*), so that regeneration can resume when conditions return.
- **Energy transduction:** moving energy from a source onto a carrier that the system's production then consumes, through means the system produces and renews. It concerns the form of energy; production consuming energy in the carriers that arrive, however catalyzed, is organizational regeneration (see Elaborations, *Energy transduction*).
- **Environmental protection:** reducing harm to the productive network from outside influences (damage to its components, diversion of its production, or loss of its medium or heat to the surroundings), through means that block, absorb, neutralize, sequester, or export those influences before they harm its components.
- **Environmental responsiveness:** adjusting activity in response to conditions in the surroundings, through a component whose state tracks an outside condition: one the system does not produce, wherever it is located.
- **Heat gain:** holding the temperature at the system's components above that of its surroundings, by heat released from the system's own energy carriers or absorbed from an outside source, through means the system produces and renews that production itself does not need where the surroundings are warm enough. Heat released by production and retained by bulk alone is organizational regeneration with the environment meeting the condition (see Elaborations, *Heat gain*).
- **Individuation:** maintaining the system's cohesion, its productive components staying together, through means the system produces and renews and that production itself does not need where the environment meets the condition. Localization following from the production relations and diffusion alone, as in a reaction-diffusion spot, is organizational regeneration with the environment meeting the condition (see Elaborations, *Individuation*). Distinction from other systems and from the surroundings is set by production relations (see *System* and *Environment*), not by individuation.
- **Resource provisioning:** making resources (materials and energy sources) available to the system where and when its production needs them.
- **Self-regulation:** compensating for variation by changing activity in response to the levels of the system's own components or by-products, including variation caused by an outside change, through an interaction that production itself does not need.
- **Waste management:** preventing by-products of the system's own activity from accumulating in forms or amounts that impair continuation, by conversion, sequestration, or export.

### Combinations of the living system

- **Energy storage:** resource provisioning (storage) combined with energy transduction, holding energy in a form charged and later discharged by transduction. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Regulated waste management:** self-regulation combined with waste management, increasing conversion or export as by-products build up. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Responsive dormancy:** environmental responsiveness combined with dormancy, entering or leaving it on a tracked outside condition. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Responsive protection:** environmental responsiveness combined with environmental protection, switching on protective means when a tracked outside condition rises. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Thermoregulation:** heat gain combined with environmental protection (insulation), waste management (shedding the system's own heat), and self-regulation or environmental responsiveness, holding the temperature at the components within a range. A combination, not a separate elaboration.

### Elaborations of the lineage

- **Dispersal:** carrying new units to sites where they can continue, beyond the reach of transport in the surroundings.
- **Partitioning:** distributing components between new units so that each receives a complete working set.

### Combinations of the lineage

- **Bet-hedging:** dormancy (or, as in persister cells, inactivity reached by self-regulation) combined with reproduction, in which offspring resume at different times, so that the lineage is spread across times. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Directed dispersal:** environmental responsiveness combined with dispersal, carrying new units toward sites marked by a tracked outside condition. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Division timed to growth:** self-regulation combined with reproduction, dividing once the system's own components suffice for each new unit. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Dormant dispersal:** dormancy combined with dispersal, so that new units outlast a transit longer than their unpreserved components survive. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Provisioning offspring:** resource provisioning (storage) combined with reproduction, supplying new units with reserves for their establishment. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Segregating damage:** waste management (sequestration) combined with reproduction, gathering damaged components so that division leaves them mainly with one new unit. A combination, not a separate elaboration.
- **Transgenerational induction:** environmental responsiveness combined with reproduction, in which a condition tracked by the parent switches means in new units, as in CRISPR immunity. A combination, not a separate elaboration.

### Elaborations of the evolving lineage

- **Error correction:** detecting and correcting errors in heritable components against a second copy of the same information, so that transmission to new units is reliable. The same repair in a system that will not reproduce keeps a working component working and is organizational regeneration (see Elaborations, *Environmental protection*).
- **Recombination:** combining heritable variants from different lineages in one system, by horizontal transfer or, in sexual reproduction, at reproduction.

### Combinations of the evolving lineage

- **Sexual reproduction:** recombination combined with reproduction, in which a new unit is formed from heritable components of two parents. A combination, not a separate elaboration.

### Specializations of heredity

- **Unlimited heredity:** heredity that can support vastly more possible heritable variants than there are systems in the population. A specialization of heredity, not a separate elaboration. It is a count at one time, met by a template of fixed modest length; open-ended evolution needs more, heritable information that can be added to over time (Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*).
