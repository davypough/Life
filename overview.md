# A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes: Study Overview

This study explores one possible answer to the question **What is life?** Its working hypothesis is that every living system **rebuilds the means of its own continuation**. That is, its processes produce and renew the components and relations that enable those processes to continue. This base capability is called **organizational regeneration**.

Starting from that proposal, the study then asks how that basic life process is elaborated under different environmental conditions mimicking evolution. The result is a functional dependency hierarchy that makes these base capabilities, elaborations, and relationships explicit. It is primarily a constructive conceptual study intended to complement what the historical record of life on Earth shows.

## Purpose and goals

- Clarify organizational regeneration as a proposed criterion for life.
- Identify distinct base capabilities and elaborations at a useful level of generality, separating them from their particular biological realizations.
- Explain what each base capability and elaboration contributes and which conditions its environment meets.
- Discover likely dependency chains, branches, and combinations of base capabilities and elaborations, where justified.
- Distinguish what remaining alive depends on from what reproduction and heredity depend on.
- Use existing biological systems and lineages to illustrate and test the proposals.

## Current direction

The study rests on a **continuation principle**: a pattern whose components are lost continues only while renewal keeps pace with loss. A living system is proposed to satisfy it by organizational regeneration, renewal through its own processes. This contrasts with a persistent process like combustion, which makes more of the radicals and heat that carry it but produces no component that enables its steps while persisting through them. The criterion is a stipulation marking this distinct way of persisting, not a match with ordinary usage, so it admits thin cases such as catalytic networks in vitro, listed as accepted consequences (Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*). For living systems, the principle applies at three levels of continuation, each with its own base capability:

| Level | Base capability | Potential elaborations |
|---|---|---|
| Living system | Organizational regeneration (proposed criterion for life) | Individuation, resource provisioning, energy transduction, self-regulation, environmental responsiveness, environmental protection, waste management, heat gain, dormancy |
| Lineage | Reproduction | Partitioning, dispersal (provisioning offspring and bet-hedging are combinations) |
| Evolving lineage | Heredity | Error correction, recombination (sexual reproduction is a combination) |

- The base capabilities form a strict dependency chain: regeneration, then reproduction, then heredity.
- Elaborations build on a base capability; no base capability in the chain depends on them. Whether a system needs one depends on which support conditions its environment meets. An elaboration is necessary only in environments that meet its condition less than the base capability can make good, and net beneficial in a wider range of them; only definitional dependencies hold across the stated range of realizations (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*; Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*).
- Systems sharing an environmental container are told apart by organizational separation, which comes in degrees. Reproduction and heredity each use a relative threshold at a natural crossover: a new system's own production outweighs what it receives from others, and heritable components pass mainly from parent to offspring rather than between lineages. Near a threshold the outcome is indeterminate, without weakening the dependency chain (Glossary, *Organizational separation*; Method, *Dependency criterion*).
- Living systems are graded and can be nested: being one living system comes in degrees with separation, and a collective that regenerates a shared organization, such as a biofilm with its matrix, is a living system for that organization, containing living systems for theirs. Being alive at this level asks little, so a lake or the biosphere is a thin living system; collective individuality lies in collective reproduction and heredity (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*). There is no level beyond the evolving lineage: levels are forms of continuation, each assessed for a stated organization, so a collective individual is the three levels assessed for a collective's organization; a community of lineages without collective reproduction carries no heredity of its own; and culture is heredity only for collectives that reproduce and pass it mainly from parent to offspring collectives, and otherwise the evolvability of components (Basis, *Levels and organizations*).
- Relations are presented as separate views, each with one kind of relation: dependency, environment, and realization.
- Open-ended evolution is a possible extended endpoint, an outcome rather than a base capability or elaboration, judged by continued production of new heritable mechanisms. It needs unlimited heredity, heritable information that can be added to and can specify new mechanisms, error correction beyond a stated range, and new conditions or environments that keep becoming available, which other lineages can supply; recombination, raised variation, dormant stages, and collective heredity are net beneficial to it. The conditions are sufficient in a sense tested by cases, holding for the cases assessed; sufficiency in general is an empirical question beyond the study's scope (Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*).

These are working proposals, not a settled set. The order reflects prerequisites, not presumed sophistication or historical age. Biological lineages can illustrate combinations and elaborations, but their history does not by itself establish the order.

## Reading and developing the study

Suggested reading order:

1. **Basis** (basis.md): the continuation principle, regeneration as the proposed criterion for life, the three levels, base capabilities, and their elaborations.
2. **Method** (method.md): approach, environmental assumptions, minimal realizations, the dependency criterion, and checks against biology.
3. **Views:** the dependency view (dependencies.md) (base capability depends on base capability), the environment view (environment.md) (base capability needs support condition, met by the environment or an elaboration), and the realization view (realization.md) (mechanism provides base capability or elaboration), which also introduces the two example systems used throughout.
4. **Base Capabilities** (capabilities.md) and **Elaborations** (elaborations.md): reference accounts of each base capability and of each elaboration, grouped by level.
5. **Life's Future** (lifes-future.md): a speculative companion applying the framework to the future of life; it is not part of the hierarchy.

The Glossary (glossary.md) holds all definitions. The reading list and the full text of Stegemann's *Primer* are in the sources folder (sources/reading-list.md, sources/primer-complete.md).

Each substantive claim has a primary home in one of these files, and they develop together as individual analyses test the basis and method. This overview changes when the study's direction, major conclusions, or principal unresolved issues change.
