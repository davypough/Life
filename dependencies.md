# Dependency View

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This view presents one relation among one kind of entity: **base capabilities** related by **depends on**. It identifies the base capabilities on which the rest of the study builds and justifies why they, and only they, appear here. Other relations, such as those between base capabilities and environmental conditions or between mechanisms and the base capabilities or elaborations they provide, belong to separate views.

## Diagram

```
Organizational regeneration        a living system continues
            │
            ▼
Reproduction                       a lineage continues
            │
            ▼
Heredity                           an evolving lineage continues
```

An arrow from A to B means **B depends on A**. Each base capability defines a level, identified by what continues at that level (right-hand column).

## The relation

B **depends on** A in the sense of the dependency criterion (Method). This view includes only dependencies that follow from what the base capabilities mean (definitional dependencies). Physical and conditional dependencies are left out until they are established.

## Why these base capabilities

Each base capability defines one level of continuation (see Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). Each is justified here by what it depends on and why the dependency is strict.

### Organizational regeneration

- **Depends on:** nothing within this study. It is the proposed criterion for life.
- **Justification:** see Basis, *Organizational regeneration as the proposed criterion for life*; the account is in Base Capabilities, *Organizational regeneration (proposed criterion for life)*.

### Reproduction

- **Depends on regeneration:** a new system counts as living only if it regenerates its own organization. Producing copies without regeneration is not reproduction of a living system. A new system, rather than an extension of the parent, is one whose organization is regenerated mainly by its own processes rather than the parent's (organizational separation; see the Glossary, *System*).
- **Strictness:** regeneration can occur without reproduction. A system may persist for some time without originating new systems, and some never do: a mule, almost always sterile, and workers of castes without ovaries, as in fire ants, regenerate throughout their lives. A worker is assessed as a member; its colony reproduces as a collective through its queen (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*).

### Heredity

- **Depends on reproduction:** transmission across generations presupposes generations.
- **Strictness:** reproduction can occur without heredity. New systems may resemble their parent only in being living systems, as when fragments of different composition separate at random. No living system of this sort is known, but model compositional networks whose offspring carry their parent's composition only weakly come close,[^vasas2010] as do biofilms that shed single cells, whose new mats carry collective variants only weakly (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*).
- **Variation:** no separate base capability or elaboration is needed. Imperfect transmission supplies variation in any physical system, so even clonal lineages vary (see the environment view, *Heredity*). Heredity without any variation is an idealization that cannot occur.

## What this view leaves out, and why

- **Elaborations.** Elaborations of regeneration, of reproduction (partitioning, dispersal), and of heredity (error correction, recombination) build on a base capability rather than being prerequisites of it, so they belong in the environment view.
- **Individuation.** Reproduction needs new systems separated from their parent, but not systems that produce their own boundary. Separation is drawn by production relations (Glossary, *System*), and the environment can meet it, as when slow diffusion between mineral pores separates networks or a reaction-diffusion spot splits (see the environment view, *Reproduction*, and Base Capabilities, *Reproduction*). Individuation is therefore one way of meeting this support condition, not a prerequisite of reproduction.
- **Growth.** Reproduction needs production exceeding losses on average across generations, not at each reproduction event, and growth is regeneration producing beyond replacement rather than a base capability. It is a support condition (see the environment view, *Reproduction*, and Base Capabilities, *Reproduction*).
- **Recombination.** It depends on heredity, while heredity occurs without it, so it is an elaboration of heredity rather than a base capability (see Elaborations, *Recombination*).
- **Unlimited heredity.** This is a specialization of heredity, not a separate elaboration that depends on it.
- **Open-ended evolution.** An outcome, not a base capability or elaboration (see Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). Its conditions are stated as necessary for or net beneficial to it, not as dependencies (Method, *Dependency criterion*; Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*).
- **Further levels.** A collective individual is this chain applied to a collective's organization, a community of lineages adds no continuant beyond a thin living system, and culture and directed variation are a means of inheritance, the evolvability of components, or existing elaborations, so none adds a base capability (Basis, *Levels and organizations*).

## What the view claims and does not claim

- It constrains order without determining history: in any lineage, heredity cannot appear before reproduction, nor reproduction before regeneration, although they may appear together.
- Dependencies run in one direction only. Being alive depends only on regeneration; reproduction and heredity are further base capabilities, not conditions imposed on every living individual.
- It says nothing about which elaborations a system has, or in what order they arise. That depends on the environment and belongs to other views.

## Open questions

None at present. What open-ended evolution needs, and how the conditions of an outcome are represented, are in Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*, and Method, *Dependency criterion*.

The sufficiency of organizational regeneration as a criterion for life is in Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*; how sharply systems must be separated is set out in the Glossary, *Organizational separation*.
