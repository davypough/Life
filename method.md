# Method

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This file develops the study's method: its approach, environmental assumptions, and tests for identifying base capabilities and elaborations, and for assessing dependencies. The conceptual basis is in Basis, definitions are in the Glossary, and the accounts are in Base Capabilities and Elaborations. These parts can develop together: a difficulty exposed by a particular base capability or elaboration may require a revision of the method, and vice versa.

## Approach

- Base capabilities and elaborations arise from organized physical and chemical processes in living systems.
- Organizational regeneration provides the starting point. Further base capabilities and elaborations contribute to maintaining a system, originating new systems, or sustaining evolving lineages.
- For each base capability and elaboration, the analysis asks what it achieves, how a minimal realization could achieve it, which support conditions the environment still meets, and which others it depends on.
- Environments are described through relevant conditions (such as resource availability, containment, damaging influences, and variability) rather than referencing particular periods of Earth's history.
- Base capabilities and elaborations are arranged by their prerequisites. The resulting partial-order hierarchy may contain dependency chains, independent branches, and elaborations that combine several others.
- Biological systems and lineages provide examples and tests of these proposals. But their historical relationships are not part of this study.

## Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations

- Each base capability and elaboration should identify a distinct contribution at a level of generality that makes its prerequisites clear.
- A base capability or elaboration should be distinguished from the particular physical means that provide it in a system (see the realization view).
- A specialized application is not automatically another step in a dependency chain: UV protection is a case of environmental protection, whereas responsive protection combines protection with environmental responsiveness.
- A specialized application takes over the same contribution from the environment as the broader base capability or elaboration, as UV protection and toxin protection both take over shelter. Contributions that take over different ones are distinct elaborations, even where they meet one support condition, as heat gain and insulation do, or share mechanisms, as environmental protection and waste management do.
- A combination is identified by four points. Its parts each occur without the others. One part times, places, or distributes another's contribution, as environmental responsiveness switches on protection or reproduction places stores in new units. A minimal realization adds no produced means beyond those of its parts. Every support condition it meets, in whole or in part, is met by a part's own contribution applied where the linking places it, including conditions of the base capability it is linked to and of new units. A base capability is a part only where the other part is not one of its own elaborations, which already act through it. A candidate that adds a produced means is assessed as an elaboration, by the takeover test above; one whose parts are not linked is a specialized application of each part, or several elaborations provided by one mechanism (realization view). The takeover test sorts specialized applications from distinct elaborations; it does not reclassify combinations, whose parts' contributions, once linked, may meet a condition no part meets alone, as bet-hedging spreads the lineage across times by dormancy.
- **Grain.** An elaboration is at a useful grain when it names one contribution taken over from the environment: one thing the surroundings would otherwise do, as listed in the environment view's "Met by the environment" entries, such as confinement, supply, charged carriers, warmth, shelter, and carrying away. Its minimal realization, with the environment meeting every other support condition, provides it by a produced means of its own that is not part of production (see *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*), and shows it taking over something no other elaboration takes over; otherwise it is combined with that elaboration, as a specialized application of it or of a broader one. The forms it covers take over the same contribution, differing only in the influence, resource, timing, or route they apply to; otherwise it is divided. And it is not two parts linked (the combination test, above). One entry can meet several support conditions, as confinement does for individuation, and one condition can hold two entries, as warmth (heat gain) and shelter from loss of heat (protection) both meet the condition on the medium and physical range. Overlap in other environments is allowed: an elaboration may meet a condition by another route, as one condition can be met by several elaborations (environment view, *Observations*).
- Shared mechanisms, co-occurrence, and diversity of mechanisms are evidence for applying the grain test, not tests. Two contributions that could not be provided by separate mechanisms in any realization can only be ones that take over the same contribution, which the takeover test already decides: for contributions that take over different ones, an environment needing only one can be stated, and its minimal realization provides that one alone. Read as a claim about known systems only, it is co-occurrence, which never suffices (*Checks against biology*). A contribution provided by unrelated mechanisms across realizations is thereby identified by its contribution rather than by a mechanism, but is not shown distinct from other elaborations, since specialized applications, such as UV and toxin protection, are also provided by unrelated mechanisms.
- A base capability is at its grain by defining a level (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*), and is divided only where a form of it defines a further form of continuation, not merely a further continuant such as a collective individual (Basis, *Levels and organizations*). Forms of reproduction, such as fission, budding, fragmentation, and spot splitting, differ in how separation and growth are met, and means of inheritance, such as composition, templates, epigenetic states, and, for collectives, culture, differ in capacity and in conditional relations of their realizations. These differences are recorded in the environment and realization views; none defines a further form of continuation.
- The current candidates and their status are recorded in Elaborations. Their inclusion is assessed using these criteria.

## Environmental conditions

- The analysis generalizes environments while retaining physical and chemical constraints.
- For each proposed realization, it specifies which conditions the environment meets and what the system contributes.
- Whether an elaboration is necessary for continuation, net beneficial, or a net cost depends on how far the environment meets the support condition it takes over (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*); the thresholds are stated in each account (see *Stating environmental assumptions*, below).
- A condition necessary for continuation does not automatically imply an elaboration the system must itself possess. The environment may meet that condition.
- Elaborations can change a system's environmental needs without removing its dependence on the environment.

## Minimal realizations

- In developed systems, base capabilities and elaborations may be tightly integrated and mutually supporting.
- Considering simpler realizations helps distinguish prerequisites of a base capability or elaboration from features of a particular implementation.
- Minimal realizations must state their environmental assumptions. There need not be a unique minimal realization, across environments or within one: minimal means having no part the base capability or elaboration does not need, not having the fewest parts.
- A base capability or elaboration counts as the system's own contribution only when the system produces and renews the relevant means. A condition met by the environment must be recorded as an environmental contribution.

### Stating environmental assumptions

Environmental assumptions are bounded as follows, so that minimal realizations can be compared and dependency claims assessed.

- **Starting environments.** Each base capability's minimal realization states how the environment meets each of that base capability's support conditions: for regeneration, the single pore (Base Capabilities, *Organizational regeneration*, *Prerequisites*); for reproduction, the pore field (Base Capabilities, *Reproduction*); for heredity, the pore field with compositional inheritance (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*).
- **One departure.** An elaboration's minimal realization departs from its base capability's minimal realization in one support condition, the one it takes over, and states the environment's shortfall there relative to what the base capability can make good. It also states how the environment meets the elaboration's own support conditions (environment view, *Observations*), such as the pore holding the pigment. Everything else is as in the starting environment and need not be restated.
- **Range.** Each account's *Environmental dependencies and limits* states where the elaboration is necessary for continuation, where it is a net cost because the environment meets the condition, and where it ceases to suffice (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*). The thresholds are relative to the system's own rates, as for organizational separation, so they apply to any chemistry. Where an elaboration meets a condition only by timing, its thresholds are those of timing (environment view, *Observations*).
- **Base capabilities.** The distinction between necessary and net beneficial does not apply to base capabilities. Each is necessary for continuation at its own level by definition and at no level below it (dependency view), and the environment cannot take it over, since propagation by chance formation or by a host's processes is not reproduction (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). The distinction applies to their support conditions, through the elaborations that meet them.
- **Dependency claims.** Because necessity is relative to an environment, it never yields a dependency of a base capability on an elaboration. What holds across the stated range of realizations is the definitional dependencies (the dependency view, and each elaboration on its base capability) and support conditions that hold in every realization, such as growth on average and the parts only the environment can meet. Relations among elaborations are conditional and are recorded in each account's Prerequisites (see *Conditional relations among elaborations*, below).

## Dependency criterion

- B depends on A if B cannot occur without A, while A can occur without B, within the stated scope of the analysis.
- Minimal realizations are used to examine these claims and seek counterexamples.
- Dependencies are definitional or physical in ground, and conditional or unconditional in scope (see the Glossary, *Relations*). Physical dependencies require supporting arguments.
- The criterion applies to each occurrence of a base capability. A condition needed only on average across a lineage, such as growth for reproduction, is not a dependency. A condition that is not itself a base capability or elaboration, even when it holds across all realizations, is a support condition and is recorded in the environment view.
- An outcome, such as open-ended evolution, is not an occurrence of a base capability or elaboration and is graded over an interval, so the criterion does not apply to it. Its conditions are stated as necessary for or net beneficial to the outcome, for a stated environment and interval, each with its ground and scope, as for elaborations and continuation (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*); they do not enter the dependency view (Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*). Whether the conditions suffice is tested by cases (see *Testing the conditions of an outcome*, below).
- Where an occurrence turns on a graded condition, such as organizational separation for reproduction, the criterion applies to determinate occurrences. Near the threshold, whether reproduction occurred may be indeterminate; regeneration holds whatever the count of systems, and heredity among units whose separation is indeterminate is itself indeterminate. Indeterminacy passes down the chain, so it yields no counterexample to a dependency.
- The study distinguishes:
  - A base capability depending on another base capability (dependency view).
  - An environment making an elaboration necessary for continuation (environment view; thresholds in each account, see *Stating environmental assumptions*).
  - A mechanism providing a base capability or elaboration in a particular system (realization view).
  - A specialized base capability or elaboration being a narrower case of a broader one.
- Functional dependency constrains possible sequences of acquisition without establishing an actual historical sequence.

### Strict dependencies between levels

- Only definitional dependencies enter the dependency view. Physical dependencies are added only once established.
- These dependencies run between levels, through the base capabilities: reproduction depends on organizational regeneration, and heredity depends on reproduction.
- Each elaboration also depends definitionally on the base capability it elaborates: its contribution meets a support condition of that base capability, which it cannot do where the base capability does not occur. These dependencies are recorded in each account's Prerequisites ("Elaborates ...") rather than in the dependency view, which relates base capabilities only.

### Conditional relations among elaborations

- Elaborations build on a base capability rather than being prerequisites of it (see Basis, *Elaborations as additions*).
- Relations involving elaborations hold only under particular environmental conditions or for particular realizations, and are recorded with those conditions. For example, provisioning by capture needs localization, and reproduction needs new units separated from their parent; each may be met by the environment or by individuation.
- Such relations do not enter the dependency chain unless they are shown to hold across the stated range of realizations.
- Pairs with no established relation in either direction remain unordered. Most such pairs are shown independent by minimal realizations, each occurring without the other, so the claim is only as strong as those realizations are plausible (see *Checks against biology*). Where no such realization exists, absence of an established relation does not demonstrate independence.

## Heuristic for identifying elaborations

- Ask what presently allows an organization-regenerating system to continue, and what happens when a supporting environmental condition is absent or changes.
- Identify a possible contribution by the system that addresses the resulting limitation.
- Determine whether that contribution is a distinct elaboration, an application of an existing one, or a combination of elaborations.
- Separate an elaboration from regeneration by asking whether the contribution is part of the production steps themselves, such as catalysis or fixed properties of the production relations, or a produced and renewed means that production itself does not need where the environment meets the condition. Self-regulation, energy transduction, individuation, and dormancy are drawn this way (see Elaborations).
- A produced means that changes what a support condition requires, rather than meeting it, takes over nothing from the environment and is a change in what regeneration produces, whether it alters components or adds to them: durable variants of A or B lower the protection a network needs, and added stabilizing solutes such as trimethylamine N-oxide widen the pressure range its chemistry permits without changing the pressure at its components (see Elaborations, *Heat gain*).
- Examine its prerequisites separately. Addressing an environmental limitation does not by itself establish a dependency on another base capability or elaboration.
- Reproduction and heredity additionally direct attention to continuation across systems and generations.

## Completeness of the elaborations

Whether an elaboration is missing is decided by takeover: one is missing where a produced means takes over a contribution that no current elaboration takes over (Glossary, *Elaboration* and *Taking over*). A support condition with no elaboration, and a support condition itself missing, are checked first, since either can expose or hide such a means.

1. **Conditions.** At each level, every loss in Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*, and everything renewal draws on, is covered by a support condition, stated by what it achieves rather than by its means or timing (environment view, *Observations*).
2. **Coverage.** Each support condition has an elaboration entry in the environment view, or a principled reason for none: the environment always meets it in full, so nothing can take it over (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*), or only the environment can meet it (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*).
3. **Candidates.** Each candidate is assessed for a stated organization, member or collective, in this order: whether it is part of production or changes what a condition requires (organizational regeneration; see *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*, above); the combination test; the takeover test, which sorts specialized applications from distinct elaborations; whether one means provides several elaborations (a mechanism; realization view); and whether it meets a support condition at any level (otherwise out of scope).
4. **Closure.** The result holds for the candidates assessed. It cannot show that no further elaboration is possible, just as the absence of a mechanism in the realization view shows only that none has been specified.

The candidates assessed so far and their results are in Elaborations, *Candidates assessed*.

## Format of each account

- **Added capability or elaboration:** the distinct contribution; for a base capability, what it adds to the more elementary form of continuation at its level.
- **How the system achieves it:** a proposed minimal realization, stating its environmental assumptions as in *Stating environmental assumptions*, and a developed example.
- **Environmental dependencies and limits:** which conditions the environment meets, what limitation the base capability or elaboration addresses, and, for an elaboration, where it is necessary for continuation, where it is a net cost, and where it ceases to suffice.
- **Prerequisites:** the base capabilities and elaborations it depends on, the grounds for each dependency, and unresolved questions.

An account is complete when, read with the views, each section does what is listed above: the contribution names the entry it takes over in the environment view (*Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*, grain); the minimal realization departs from the starting environment in one support condition, stating the environment's shortfall there and how the environment meets the elaboration's own support conditions (*Stating environmental assumptions*); a developed example is given; *Environmental dependencies and limits* states where the elaboration is necessary for continuation, where it is a net cost, and where it ceases to suffice, relative to the system's own rates or as thresholds of timing; Prerequisites state that it elaborates its base capability, each conditional relation with what can meet it, and each pair with another elaboration of the same base capability as related or unordered, in one of the two accounts; and the environment view has an entry for it, and the realization view a row in each example system that can provide it. For a base capability, its support conditions take the place of thresholds (*Stating environmental assumptions*, base capabilities). Accounts carry no status label; all are conjectural, as the hierarchy is (*Scope and limitations*).

## Checks against biology

- Examine whether proposed realizations are physically and chemically plausible.
- Compare different biological implementations of the same contribution.
- Use minimal cells and organisms dependent on hosts to examine how contributions are divided between a system and its environment.
- Seek cases in which a proposed prerequisite is absent, met by the environment, or achieved differently.
- Use biological lineages to illustrate combinations and elaborations, distinguishing functional interpretation from evidence about historical order.
- Universality is relevant when evaluating the proposed criterion for life: no living system may lack regeneration (Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*). It is not required of every elaboration.

### Testing relations against cases

A case illustrates, qualifies, or counts against a relation according to the kind of relation (Glossary, *Relations*). First, the case is assessed for a stated organization, member or collective, and at a stated level; it is confirmed to be a living system, suspended or not (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*); and an occurrence near a threshold is set aside, since indeterminacy yields no counterexample (*Dependency criterion*, above). Systems that propagate or carry variants without being living systems, such as crystals, viruses, and prions, test where the definitions draw their lines rather than the relations themselves (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). Candidates for the criterion for life itself are tested as in *Testing the criterion for life*, below.

- **Definitional dependencies,** between base capabilities or of an elaboration on its base capability. A case counts against one only if it determinately meets the study's own definition of the dependent while the prerequisite is determinately absent; ordinary usage, as in saying that viruses reproduce, is no test. Since the relation follows from meaning, the remedy for a real counterexample is to restate a definition, not to weaken the chain. Any determinate occurrence of the prerequisite without the dependent illustrates the strictness of the relation; biological cases are preferred to hypothetical ones.
- **Conditional relations.** A case in the stated range of realizations illustrates the relation when the support condition is met by one of the listed alternatives, the environment or an elaboration. It qualifies the relation when the condition is met in a way not listed, which is then added, or when the range is drawn wrongly. It counts against the relation when the dependent occurs in that range with the condition not met. A relation found to hold across all realizations becomes a candidate physical dependency and needs a supporting argument.
- **Unordered claims.** Evidence that each of a pair occurs without the other comes in three strengths: a minimal realization, which is hypothetical; the mechanism shown working in vitro without the other, which makes that realization plausible; and a living system with one and not the other. A pair that co-occurs in every known living system, supported only by a minimal realization, is recorded as a coupling (realization view, *Observations*). A case counts against an unordered claim only where co-occurrence is joined by a physical argument that one contribution cannot be made without the other and every proposed dissociation fails on physical grounds. Co-occurrence alone never suffices, as transduction and the membrane show.

This procedure is also what would establish, qualify, or defeat a proposed physical dependency. Case results have their homes with the relations they test: the strictness of the chain in the dependency view; propagation and evolvability without life in Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*; couplings in the realization view, *Observations*; and conditional relations and unordered claims in the Prerequisites of each account.

### Testing the criterion for life

A candidate is a living system where it determinately passes four tests, in order; a case near the threshold of any of them is indeterminate.

1. **Production:** it forms components by transforming supplied materials. Incorporating components that arrive as such is accretion.
2. **Means:** among its products are components that each enable more than one occurrence of a production step, on average, without being transformed by it, and on which its production depends (Glossary, *Organizational regeneration*). One occurrence marks the natural crossover between a catalyst and a reagent.
3. **Own production:** its means are renewed mainly by its own production (Glossary, *Organizational separation*), assessed for a stated organization, member or collective.
4. **Renewal predominates:** renewal makes good more than half of its losses, or its regeneration is suspended and its organization would resume (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*).

Ordinary usage is no test. A case that passes while persisting by supply, by durability, or by another system's processes calls for restating a definition, as for a definitional dependency (*Testing relations against cases*, above). A case that passes and is not living in ordinary usage is an accepted consequence where any condition excluding it would also exclude a starting environment or make being alive depend on reproduction. A graded answer to a borderline case is adequate, not an evasion, where the gradient is a physical quantity relative to the system's own rates, it has a natural crossover, most cases lie far from it, and indeterminacy does not pass down the dependency chain (*Dependency criterion*, above); separation, suspension, and the crossover in the second test meet all four. The candidates assessed and the results are in Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*.

### Testing the conditions of an outcome

The conditions stated for an outcome graded over an interval, such as open-ended evolution, are necessary for it or net beneficial to it (*Dependency criterion*, above). A conceptual study can show a condition necessary, by a definitional or physical argument that the outcome fails without it, but cannot show a set of conditions sufficient, since that claims that no system needs anything further. Like the completeness of the elaborations, sufficiency can only be tested by cases.

A set of conditions is sufficient for the outcome, in the sense the study uses, where no case meets every condition throughout the stated interval while the outcome determinately fails (Glossary, *Necessary for, net beneficial to, or sufficient for an outcome*). A case is assessed in three steps.

1. **Domain:** it is an evolving lineage, or a set of lineages, of living systems (*Testing relations against cases*, above). Systems that propagate or carry variants without being living systems, such as digital organisms, are models: they can suggest a missing condition but cannot count against sufficiency.
2. **Conditions:** it meets each necessary condition throughout the interval. A lineage that is lost meets none of them from then on.
3. **Outcome:** its production of new heritable mechanisms determinately levels off over the interval. A case near that threshold is indeterminate and yields no counter-case.

A case that passes all three counts against sufficiency: it exposes a missing necessary condition, which is added with its ground and scope, or a condition stated too weakly, which is restated. A case that meets every condition and shows the outcome illustrates that the conditions are compatible with it but does not test sufficiency. A case that lacks a condition does not test sufficiency either; if it shows the outcome, it counts against that condition's necessity. The cases assessed and the results are in Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*.

## Scope and limitations

- The hierarchy is conjectural. Definitions of base capabilities and elaborations, and dependency claims, may change during the analysis.
- Generalized environments must be specified sufficiently to make proposed realizations and dependencies assessable.
- The method does not establish how life originated or reconstruct a single evolutionary sequence.
- Greater elaboration implies neither predetermined progress nor better performance in every environment.
- The distinction between the living system, the lineage, and the evolving lineage (remaining alive, reproducing, and carrying variants forward by heredity) is maintained throughout.
- Speculation about life's future, applying the framework, is in Life's Future (lifes-future.md); it does not enter the hierarchy or its views.

## Questions for developing the method

1. Does the chosen grain distinguish basic contributions from their implementations, specialized applications, and combinations? Closed: see *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations* (grain); results are recorded in the accounts in Elaborations.
2. How should environmental assumptions be bounded so that minimal realizations can be compared and dependency claims assessed? Closed: see *Minimal realizations*, *Stating environmental assumptions*; the distinction between necessary and net beneficial is in Basis, *Logic of elaboration*, and the results are in each account.
3. What evidence would establish, qualify, or defeat a proposed physical dependency? Closed: see *Checks against biology*, *Testing relations against cases*.

The sufficiency of the criterion for life is settled in Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*, with its test in *Testing the criterion for life*, above. Questions about particular base capabilities and elaborations belong with the relevant discussions in Base Capabilities and Elaborations.

## Developing the study incrementally

Each substantive claim has a primary home. The conceptual basis is in Basis, definitions in the Glossary, analytical criteria here, relations in the views, and the accounts of base capabilities and elaborations in Base Capabilities and Elaborations. Cross-references connect them without repeating the full discussions. Literature is cited by named Markdown footnotes, such as `[^rajamani2010]`, giving author and year; the full reference is kept only in the reading list (sources/reading-list.md), and footnote definitions are placed at the end of each file.

Work can proceed by specifying an environmental assumption, developing a base capability or elaboration, and examining its prerequisites. If that analysis exposes a methodological problem, the method is revised before the affected claims are reassessed. The overview is updated when the direction, major conclusions, or principal unresolved issues change.
