# Environment View

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This view presents the relation between **base capabilities** and the **support conditions** they need, and identifies what can meet each condition: the environment or the system itself. The base capabilities are those of the dependency view: organizational regeneration, reproduction, and heredity.

## Terms

Support condition, elaboration, and taking over are defined in the Glossary, *Environment and support*.

## Diagram

```
                                                ┌── met by the environment
Base capability ──needs──▶ Support condition ◀──┤
                                                └── met by an elaboration
```

## How the support conditions are identified

For each base capability, the question is what must hold for it to succeed:

- **Regeneration:** what must hold for production to keep pace with losses?
- **Reproduction:** what must hold for a complete new system to form and reach a site where it can continue, and for the lineage to continue across systems?
- **Heredity:** what must hold for a lineage's particular organization to be carried forward and to make a difference to its persistence?

Conditions are stated by what they achieve, not by how they are achieved. The tables give the ways each condition can be met, each with an example.

## Organizational regeneration

| Support condition | Met by the environment (category; example) | Met by an elaboration (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Components stay together (they stay dense enough where they interact for production to keep pace with losses, including loss by dispersal) | Confinement or adsorption by the surroundings, or slow transport in a still medium with fixed properties of the production relations keeping the network localized; e.g., a mineral pore, a clay surface, a reaction-diffusion spot in still water | Individuation; e.g., a membrane or binding jelly |
| Resources (materials and energy sources) are accessible | Abundant local supply; e.g., a continuous flow of reactive fluid | Resource provisioning; e.g., capture, uptake, storage, a light-harvesting antenna gathering light to a transducing means. In part: environmental responsiveness (timing production or uptake to a varying supply, as a sensing component raises A and B while a pulse of R lasts) |
| Energy is usable (it reaches the system's processes in a form they consume, enough to drive them as continuation needs) | Energy arriving in carriers production consumes; e.g., energetically favorable reactants (R), or a second energy-rich resource consumed directly in production steps | Energy transduction; e.g., a coupling component using light to turn R into activated R, ATP synthase |
| The chemistry has its medium and a physical range it permits at the components (e.g., liquid water; temperature and pressure) | A surrounding medium within the chemistry's range, or, in a large system, heat released by production and retained by bulk alone; e.g., a water-filled pore at moderate temperature, a compost heap warmed by microbial activity | Heat gain (temperature); e.g., a heating component breaking down R and releasing its energy as heat in a large, poorly conducting pore, brown fat. In part: environmental protection (holding water, heat, or solute balance against the surroundings, as a biofilm matrix holds water against drying, fur holds heat, and compatible solutes hold water against outside salt), waste management (shedding the system's own heat). A chemistry tolerating a wider range, whether by changed components or by added stabilizing solutes, is a change in what regeneration produces, not an elaboration, since it changes the range the condition requires rather than meeting it (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*), even where self-regulation switches it as conditions change, as in homeoviscous adaptation (see Elaborations, *Heat gain*, *Environmental protection*, and *Self-regulation*) |
| Component levels stay within workable proportions | Steady inputs, with fixed properties of the production relations absorbing small variation (mass action, depletion of reactants, and catalysts making one another, which pulls their ratio back); sufficient where variation is small relative to the number of copies of each component; e.g., stable flow and composition, a large network | Self-regulation; e.g., excess A inhibiting B, feedback inhibition of enzymes. In part: environmental responsiveness (responding to an outside change before it shifts proportions) |
| Harm from outside influences is limited (damage to components; diversion by parasitic components) | Shelter from, or absence of, harmful influences; e.g., overlying water or rock blocking UV, neighboring systems removing hydrogen peroxide | Environmental protection; e.g., absorbing pigments, protective coatings, neutralizing enzymes, exclusion of foreign components. In part: environmental responsiveness (switching protection on when a tracked outside condition rises; responsive protection) |
| The system's by-products do not accumulate to harmful levels | Dispersal, dilution, or neutralization; e.g., flowing water carrying residue away | Waste management; e.g., conversion, sequestration, export. In part: self-regulation (increasing conversion or export as by-products build up; regulated waste management) |
| Interruptions of the support conditions end before the organization is lost | Steady conditions, or interruptions shorter than unrenewed components survive, including any slowing of loss that follows from inactivity alone; e.g., a permanently wet pore | Dormancy; e.g., a glassy component setting around A and B as the pore dries, a dehydrated spore core. In part: environmental responsiveness or self-regulation (entering or leaving dormancy on a tracked outside condition, as in responsive dormancy, or on the system's own state) |

Regeneration also tolerates some loss and damage on its own, by replacing components. A support condition becomes limiting only when losses exceed what regeneration can replace. Whether an elaboration meeting it is then necessary for continuation, net beneficial, or a net cost is set out in Basis, *Logic of elaboration*.

## Reproduction

| Support condition | Met by the environment (category; example) | Met by an elaboration (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Production exceeds losses on average across generations (growth), supplying the material of new systems, including any growth a new unit needs after its founding | Rich supply, including where new units arrive; e.g., abundant materials and energy | Resource provisioning and energy transduction, increasing surplus; provisioning offspring (a combination of resource provisioning and reproduction), supplying a new unit's founding growth from its parent's stores |
| The system separates into new units (organizational separation: exchange between units is slow compared with each unit's own renewal) | Physical disruption, restricted transport, or the network's own production and diffusion; e.g., currents fragmenting a gel, slow diffusion into a neighboring pore, a reaction-diffusion spot splitting as its center is depleted of R | Individuation with growth and division; e.g., an overgrown membrane splitting |
| Each new unit receives a complete working set | Chance, sufficient when each new unit receives many copies of every component; e.g., large fragments | Partitioning; e.g., a component linking A to B, partition machinery. In part: self-regulation (division timed to growth, dividing once components suffice for each new unit) |
| New units reach sites where they can continue (room) | Transport in the surroundings to vacant sites within reach; e.g., diffusion into an empty pore, currents | Dispersal; e.g., produced means of release, or motility. In part: environmental responsiveness (directed dispersal, toward sites marked by a tracked outside condition), dormancy (dormant dispersal, outlasting a longer transit) |
| The lineage's systems are not all lost to one disruption (their losses are not all correlated) | Heterogeneous surroundings, with transport scattering new units, or disruptions that do not pass between systems; e.g., currents carrying founders to separate pores, a sparse population through which a phage spreads slowly | Dispersal (across sites); bet-hedging, a combination of dormancy, or inactivity reached by self-regulation, with reproduction (across times). In part: environmental protection by self-destruction of infected systems, switched by environmental responsiveness, keeping a disruption from passing between the lineage's systems (abortive infection; see Elaborations, *Candidates assessed*) |

Each new unit is also a living system, so it needs the support conditions of regeneration at its site (see *Organizational regeneration*, above). This table lists only the conditions specific to originating new systems and continuing the lineage. Combinations with reproduction can also meet regeneration's conditions for new units, as segregating damage keeps by-products low in one new unit by leaving damaged components mainly with the other. Like growth, spreading concerns the lineage rather than each new unit: room asks whether a new unit can continue, spreading whether the lineage's losses are correlated. The two come apart: ample room at one site gives no spreading, and bet-hedging spreads the lineage without new room.

## Heredity

| Support condition | Met by the environment (category; example) | Met by an elaboration (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Heritable components pass mainly from parent to offspring (vertical transmission outweighs horizontal transfer)[^woese2002] | Separated compartments with limited exchange; e.g., pores linked by slow diffusion | Individuation. In part: environmental protection (excluding foreign heritable components, as restriction enzymes do) |
| Benefits stay with the variant that produces them | Compartments limiting sharing of products; e.g., the same pores | Individuation |
| Transmission is reliable enough for the amount of heritable information (errors per transmission below the error threshold) | Conditions that limit errors, and the inherent selectivity of the chemistry; e.g., moderate temperature, low radiation, enzyme-free copying stalling after a mismatch | Error correction; e.g., proofreading, mismatch and damage repair. In part: environmental protection (limiting damage from outside), waste management (removing by-products that damage heritable components, such as reactive oxygen), partitioning (each new unit receives the variant), self-regulation (feedback holding a variant composition in place), recombination (restoring error-free combinations lost by chance in finite populations, against Muller's ratchet)[^felsenstein1974] |
| Variation arises (new variants, and combinations of variants that arose in different lineages) | New variants: disturbance and chemical noise, with the imperfection inherent in any transmission; e.g., radiation, side reactions, copying errors. Combinations: exchange of heritable components between lineages through the surroundings; e.g., slow diffusion carrying a variant of A between pores | Recombination, taking over exchange between lineages by the system's own means (combinations only). No means takes over the arising of new variants, which the imperfection inherent in transmission always supplies (see Elaborations, *Candidates assessed*). Since the environment always meets the condition through new variants, recombination is never necessary for it (see Elaborations, *Recombination*) |

Two parents are both vertical sources, so sexual reproduction meets the first condition, and horizontal transfer below the threshold is compatible with it; recombination works within it (see Elaborations, *Recombination*). In the compositional pore field, where the heritable components are the organization's own and diffusion moves all components alike, this condition is stricter than separation for reproduction, because a generation lasts at least one renewal time. Elsewhere the two come apart: cells exchanging genes by horizontal transfer remain separate systems. Applied to collectives, with members, or states they carry such as culture, in place of heritable components, the first two conditions decide whether a collective carries heredity of its own (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*). The selectivity of a produced copying catalyst, as when a polymerase favors correct pairs, belongs to the means of inheritance, as catalysis belongs to production, so it is neither the environment's share of reliable transmission nor an elaboration (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*).

Failure of carrier systems, the third loss at this level (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*), needs no further condition. The systems carrying a variant are systems of the lineage, so their losses are met by reproduction's support conditions applied to them: growth and room, so that carriers are replaced as fast as they are lost, and spreading, so that one disruption does not remove them all (see *Reproduction*, above). Dispersal, bet-hedging, and dormant stages therefore carry variants forward through those conditions. Chance loss of the least error-laden carriers in small populations becomes irreversible only through transmission errors, so it is met under reliable transmission (Muller's ratchet; see the table above). Carriers of harmful variants lost by selection are not a loss of the lineage's particular variants in this sense, since selection is what heredity enables.

## Conditions only the environment can meet

Part of some support conditions can be met only by the environment. The tables state support conditions at the system's components: what reaches them and what they are exposed to. Elaborations act there, because every means is a component the system produces from what arrives and renews within its surroundings. No produced means can change the surroundings themselves: what they contain, the medium and physical range they offer, and how they change in time and space. That part of a condition stays with the environment and cannot be taken over. For materials and energy the ground is also physical: production transforms matter and consumes a disequilibrium, but creates neither.

At the level of the living system, these parts are:

- Raw materials and an energy source exist in the surroundings. An energy source is a disequilibrium, such as light, a redox pair, a gradient, or energy-rich compounds. This is the environment's share of the conditions that resources are accessible and that energy is usable: resource provisioning improves access, and energy transduction the form.
- A medium, and a physical range from which the system's means can hold its components within the chemistry's range, exist in the surroundings; e.g., liquid water, temperature, and pressure. This is the environment's share of the condition that the chemistry has its medium and range at the components: protection holds water, heat, and solute balance, and heat gain holds temperature above that of the surroundings, only as far as the surroundings allow, and heat gain draws on the energy source above; a chemistry tolerating a wider range is a change in what regeneration produces.
- Suitable conditions return after an interruption. This is the environment's share of the condition that interruptions end before the organization is lost: dormancy lengthens the interruption a system survives but cannot bring conditions back.

At the level of the lineage, one part is added:

- Disruptions spare some of the sites or times the lineage's systems occupy. This is the environment's share of the condition that the lineage's systems are not all lost to one disruption: dispersal and bet-hedging spread the lineage over more sites and times but cannot make the surroundings spare any.

Room is not a further part: a site where a new unit can continue is one where regeneration's conditions hold, including the parts above, and dispersal extends reach but cannot create a site (Elaborations, *Dispersal*). Heredity adds none: variation follows from imperfect transmission, vertical transmission and benefits staying with their producers can be met wholly by individuation, and reliable transmission by error correction with the elaborations that meet it in part. Recombination's need for lineages carrying different variants, and for contact between them, is a support condition of recombination, met by reproduction, variation, and the surroundings (Elaborations, *Recombination*).

These parts are support conditions, not limits on the scope of the study. A condition that is not a base capability or elaboration, even when it holds across all realizations, is a support condition (Method, *Dependency criterion*), and the failure of one of these parts is analysed within the study: the system runs down, or a dormant system is living until its organization is lost but does not continue (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*). Which parts stay with the environment is assessed for a stated system: a collective can meet part of a condition for its members, as a matrix shields cells from drying, while the collective has surroundings of its own.

## Observations

- **One elaboration can meet several conditions.** Individuation keeps components together, separates new units, keeps lineages apart, and keeps benefits with their producers. Environmental protection limits harm from outside influences and holds the medium and heat at the components, both by shelter (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*). Partitioning completes new units and carries heritable variants into each. It appeared to be a prerequisite in earlier drafts partly for this reason, and partly because being one system was not distinguished from maintaining a boundary. Systems are told apart by their production relations (Glossary, *System*), and the environment can keep them separate, including through the network's own production and diffusion, as when a reaction-diffusion spot splits.
- **One condition can be met by several elaborations.** Component proportions are kept workable by self-regulation and, in part, by environmental responsiveness; harm from outside influences is limited by environmental protection and, in part, by responsiveness switching protection on.
- **Control meets conditions by timing.** Self-regulation and environmental responsiveness change activity on a tracked level: self-regulation on the system's own components or by-products, after they shift; responsiveness on a condition the system does not produce, possibly before that condition acts on internal levels. Beyond keeping proportions workable, each meets part of other conditions by timing the production or means that meet them, as the combinations show (Elaborations, *Combinations at the living-system level*). Timing is how a condition is met, not what it achieves, so it is not a further support condition. Responsiveness makes a difference where outside changes act faster than internal levels shift, as with brief pulses of R or of a toxin, and where the means it switches cannot be kept on at a cost the balance allows, or cannot be kept on at all, as with dormancy. The environment meets the timing where conditions are steady, where change is slow enough for internal levels to follow, where a means with fixed properties can be kept on, or where the change itself triggers such a means, as drying sets the glassy component in minimal dormancy.
- **Elaborations have support conditions of their own.** A membrane or a transport mechanism must itself be produced and renewed, so taking over a condition shifts dependence on the environment rather than removing it, ultimately onto the parts only the environment can meet (see *Conditions only the environment can meet*, above).
- **Meeting a condition is a matter of degree.** The environment and the system may each meet part of a condition, and the balance can change with circumstances.
- **Some conditions need hold only on average.** Growth need not occur at each reproduction event, only across generations (see Base Capabilities, *Reproduction*).
- **Freedom from interference is not a further support condition.** Interference from outside, such as toxins or parasitic components that divert production, is harm from outside influences. Side reactions within the system either consume materials and components, a loss regeneration replaces, or yield by-products that impair continuation. Parasitic variants within an evolving lineage are met by the condition that benefits stay with the variant that produces them. Environmental protection and waste management each meet part of it (see Elaborations).
- **Living systems make part of the environment.** Modifying the surroundings is not a further elaboration, because the line between system and environment is drawn by production, not location (Glossary, *Environment*). Its cases fall into three groups. Produced means kept as working components outside the system's other components, such as a biofilm matrix, siderophores, or a mineral shell, belong to the system and provide existing elaborations (individuation, resource provisioning, environmental protection). By-products that change conditions, such as acids lowering the pH of the medium, call for waste management where they harm the producer; where they benefit it, as by slowing competitors, the benefit reaches it only through conditions it does not produce, so the environment meets the condition. Products released and no longer renewed, such as a mineral deposit outlasting its maker, are environment, including for the producer's offspring, which inherit modified surroundings as part of their environment.[^odlingsmee2003] A product is a working component when it takes part in a process the network produces that returns something to its regeneration, raising renewal or reducing loss, as siderophores return iron through the cell's own receptors. Shared means reach neighbors that do not make them, so they meet heredity's condition that benefits stay with the variant that produces them only where the surroundings keep their effects local; otherwise non-producers spread.[^griffin2004] Means made jointly by several systems are assigned in the realization view, *Observations*.
- **New elaborations appear at the higher levels.** Partitioning and dispersal meet support conditions of reproduction, and error correction and recombination meet support conditions of heredity. Further conditions of reproduction are met by combinations linking a living-system elaboration to reproduction (provisioning offspring, division timed to growth, bet-hedging, segregating damage, transgenerational induction) or to dispersal (directed dispersal, dormant dispersal); sexual reproduction links recombination to reproduction (see Elaborations). Elaborations acting on heritable components with their own contributions, as protection, waste management, self-regulation, and partitioning do for reliable transmission, are specialized applications rather than combinations, and are recorded as in-part entries only (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).
