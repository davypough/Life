# Elaborations

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This file gives an account of each elaboration: an addition to a base capability. Accounts follow the format set out in Method, *Format of each account*, and are grouped by the level whose base capability they elaborate. Examples use the A/B network and the living cell introduced in the realization view, *Example systems*. The relations of elaborations to support conditions are presented in the environment view.

## Elaborations of the living system

These elaborations of organizational regeneration meet support conditions that the environment may otherwise meet (see the environment view, *Organizational regeneration*).

### Individuation

#### Added elaboration

Individuation-maintaining processes help keep a productive network together as a localized system. The elaboration is to **maintain the system's cohesion**: its productive components staying dense enough where they interact for production to keep pace with losses (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*). With organizational regeneration alone, the environment may meet its need for containment. With individuation, the system's own activity contributes to preserving that containment or cohesion, including perhaps through small environmental changes. Separation must still permit exchange: essential components must remain sufficiently associated for their mutually supporting processes to continue, while resources can enter and waste can leave.

Merely being separated is not the new elaboration: the productive network contributes to maintaining its own separation in addition to its functioning as a productive unit. Nor is being one system rather than two, or being distinct from the surroundings, both of which are set by production relations (Glossary, *System* and *Environment*).

- **From organizational regeneration:** a network can localize itself through its own production and diffusion rates alone, as in self-replicating reaction-diffusion spots.[^pearson1993] Where R diffuses faster than A and B, and their production falls short of losses below some density, A and B stay concentrated in spots: components leaving a spot decay outside it, and those inside are renewed. In part of that range a network spread evenly would run down, so the localization meets the condition, but it adds no means: it follows from the production relations in a still medium. Like the feedback that follows from the production relations alone (see *Self-regulation*, below), it is recorded with the fixed properties through which the environment meets the condition, sufficient only where transport in the medium is slow relative to renewal; stirring or flow dissolves the spots. Individuation begins with a produced and renewed means of cohesion that production itself does not need where the environment meets the condition: a dedicated material, such as a jelly, a membrane, or a component anchoring A and B to a mineral surface, or an added binding property of a renewed component (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*). Spatial patterns without boundaries are told apart as systems by their production relations, as with the spiral waves of a spatial hypercycle.[^boerlijst1991]

#### How the system achieves it

A system with individuation-maintaining processes renews a means of containment or cohesion, such as a physical or electrostatic boundary or an adhesive binding material. This helps prevent essential components from dispersing faster than they can be replenished. Continued renewal can compensate for modest loss or disruption of the material that keeps it together.

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies an open surface in slowly flowing water, with no confining pore, where A and B are carried off faster than they are renewed and too fast for the network to localize itself. It also produces a jelly-like material that retains A and B near one another (for example, by stickiness) while letting R reach them and residue leave; the jelly does not hold R, which would be provisioning. Their continued interaction enables further production of the jelly. If small disturbances remove some of the jelly, ongoing production restores sufficient cohesion for the network to continue. The flow still supplies R and carries residue away. The network thus maintains a condition a pore would otherwise meet.

**Developed example.** A living cell renews its membrane, which helps retain cellular components while permitting exchanges with the surroundings. Producing membrane components becomes part of organizational regeneration, and individuation takes over containment from the environment. Both become functional in the integrated system.

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

The environment supplies usable raw materials and energy, together with conditions compatible with the productive network and its means of individuation. Here we assume that resources are sufficiently available nearby and can reach the localized productive processes.

For the A/B network, the surroundings supply R, from which A, B, and the jelly can be produced. The network thus contributes to maintaining its own cohesion. In the cell example, the surroundings supply nutrients and usable energy under conditions in which membrane renewal and exchange can continue.

Environmental changes must remain within tolerable limits. The elaboration here is maintaining individuation despite some variation in the surroundings.

The environment meets the condition without individuation by confinement (a mineral pore), by adsorption (a clay surface), or by slow transport in a still medium, where the network's own production and diffusion keep it localized. A produced means of cohesion goes further than self-localization where transport is fast: a jelly fragment holds a founding set together when carried by flow, which dissolves a spot (see *Dispersal*, below). Individuation is necessary where transport carries A and B off faster than they are renewed and too fast for the network to localize itself, as on the open surface above; a net cost in a confining pore, on an adsorbing surface, or in still water where the network forms spots; and ceases to suffice where disruption removes the means of cohesion faster than its renewal restores it.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration: the means of cohesion must be produced by the productive network it holds together.
- Unordered with respect to resource provisioning, energy transduction, self-regulation, and environmental responsiveness (see *Environmental responsiveness*, below).
- Begins with a produced and renewed means of cohesion; localization that follows from the production relations and diffusion alone is met on the environment's side (see *From organizational regeneration*, above).

### Resource provisioning

#### Added elaboration

Resource-provisioning processes help make environmental resources available to a localized productive network. The elaboration is to **help secure usable resources for the system's continued activity**. Without provisioning, we assume that resources are sufficiently available nearby and can reach the productive processes. With provisioning, the system's own activity contributes to establishing or sustaining that availability. Capturing, concentrating, or retaining resources can reduce dependence on their immediate abundance in the surroundings. Resources here include energy sources as well as materials. Provisioning concerns where and when a resource is available to the system, not its form; converting energy into a usable form is a separate elaboration (energy transduction).

This elaboration concerns the supply of usable materials to the network, whereas individuation concerns keeping the network's productive components together. A single material or structure may contribute to both.

#### How the system achieves it

A system with resource-provisioning processes renews a means of making resources available to its productive network. At its simplest, provisioning need not involve sensing a shortage or adjusting activity in response: the physical properties of a continually renewed material may be sufficient.

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a mineral pore through which R arrives too dilute for production to keep pace with losses. The network also makes from R a capturing material that binds passing R loosely and stays in the pore, holding R where A and B can use it. The pore contains the network and the capturing material, supplies R and energy, and carries residue away; no individuation is needed. Replacing the capturing material with a richer supply of R leaves regeneration unchanged, with the environment meeting the condition.

**Combined example.** Suppose the jelly also captures some of the R passing through its surroundings. If the captured R remains accessible to A and B, its local retention can support production when the surrounding supply is dilute or temporarily reduced. That production, in turn, renews the jelly. The network thus helps maintain access to the resources needed for its own regeneration and cohesion. Capturing substances only contributes to provisioning if they can be used; trapping them in an inaccessible form would not accomplish provisioning.

This example combines individuation and provisioning in one material, one mechanism providing both (realization view, *Observations*).

**Developed example.** A living cell produces and renews transport proteins that enable the uptake of nutrients across its membrane. The membrane contributes to individuation, while the transport proteins contribute to resource provisioning. Both depend on the productive network that they help sustain.

Resource storage extends this elaboration across time, allowing supplies acquired under favorable conditions to support activity during later shortages. Provisioning can also support the establishment of a new system, as in the nutrient reserves supplied to developing organisms in seeds and eggs (see *Combinations at the lineage level*, below). The first is provisioning extended across time; the second is provisioning combined with reproduction.

Capture, uptake, storage, and the gathering of light by an antenna (see *Energy transduction*, below) are specialized applications of provisioning: each takes over supply where and when production needs it, differing in route, in space, or in time (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

The environment still supplies the materials and energy on which the system depends, together with conditions compatible with regeneration and provisioning. Resource-provisioning processes improve access to those supplies; they cannot compensate indefinitely for their absence. The resources obtained must be sufficient to support continued activity, including the production and renewal of the provisioning mechanism itself.

For the A/B network, the surroundings supply R, which encounters the jelly and can be retained in a usable form. The network contributes to its local availability but still depends on its arrival. In the cell example, the surroundings supply nutrients that its transport mechanisms can take up, together with usable energy sources for continued operation.

Environmental shortages must remain within tolerable limits. Capture is limited by what reaches the system, and stored resources can be exhausted. The elaboration here is helping maintain resource availability despite some variation in environmental supply. Provisioning is necessary where resources arrive too dilute or too intermittently for production to keep pace with losses, and a net cost where the supply meets the condition with margin. Between the two it is often net beneficial without being necessary, raising the surplus that growth supplies to reproduction (environment view, *Reproduction*). It ceases to suffice where what reaches the system, captured in full, falls short of what production needs, or where a shortage outlasts its stores.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration: the provisioning means must be produced by the productive network.
- Needs localization (a physical support condition): captured resources benefit the network only if they remain near its components. Localization may be met by the environment or by individuation, so provisioning does not depend on individuation.
- Unordered with respect to individuation, energy transduction, self-regulation, and environmental responsiveness.

### Energy transduction

#### Added elaboration

Energy-transducing processes move energy from a source in the surroundings onto a carrier (Glossary, *Energy carrier*) that the system's production then consumes. The elaboration is to **make available energy usable for the system's own production**. Without it, energy must arrive in a form production consumes: reactants that are energetically favorable to convert, such as R, or a second energy-rich resource consumed directly in production steps (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*).

Energy transduction concerns the form of available energy, whereas provisioning concerns where and when a resource is available. Food illustrates the difference: taking in and storing food is provisioning; extracting its energy into a carrier such as ATP is transduction; and using its carbon as building material is part of regeneration. A single resource can therefore be acted on by several base capabilities and elaborations without their overlapping.

- **From organizational regeneration:** a catalyst changes the rate of a step, not its driving force, so it couples nothing: any step it speeds must already be favorable with its reactants as they arrive. A step that consumes R together with a second energy-rich reactant is the same case, the energy arriving already in a carrier production consumes, and so is a pathway in which energy stays with the material as it is transformed. Production consuming energy in the carriers that arrive is regeneration, with the environment meeting the condition. Transduction begins with a produced and renewed charging means that production itself does not need where the environment supplies charged carriers: a dedicated coupling component, or an added property of a renewed component, such as a variant of B that absorbs light. The line is drawn as for self-regulation (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*).
- **From resource provisioning:** provisioning gathers or holds an energy source; transduction changes its form. Each occurs without the other: jelly capturing energy-rich R provisions without transducing, and a coupling component free in a lit pore transduces without provisioning. A light-harvesting antenna gathers light to the converting means. It addresses scarce light, not an unusable form, so it is provisioning, benefiting the system only through a transducing means (a conditional relation); cells reduce their antennas in bright light, while the reaction center alone still transduces.
- **From waste management:** keeping by-products low preserves the driving force of production but moves no energy onto a carrier (see *Waste management*, below).

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a lit pore where R arrives in a less reactive form, too weakly favorable to convert for production to keep pace with losses, as where networks upstream have used up the energy-rich R. The network also makes a coupling component that absorbs light and uses its energy to turn R into activated R, which B converts into A and A into B, as before; the coupling component is itself made from activated R. The pore holds the coupling component and the activated R near A and B, supplies R and light, and carries residue away; no individuation is needed. Replacing the light with a supply of activated R leaves regeneration unchanged, with the environment meeting the condition.

**Developed example.** In living cells, fermentation makes ATP from glucose by substrate-level phosphorylation, with no gradient involved. In respiration and photosynthesis, redox reactions or light build an ion gradient across the membrane, which ATP synthase converts into ATP;[^mitchell1961] in photosynthesis, a reaction center first converts the excitation of its pigment into separated charges. ATP is then consumed by many production steps.

A carrier shared by many steps, such as ATP, is a mechanism rather than a further elaboration: it meets no condition beyond the one transduction meets, and links many sources to many consumers, including the means of other elaborations (realization view, *Observations*). Energy storage is provisioning (storage) applied to an energy source; where the store is charged and discharged by transduction, it is a combination (see *Combinations at the living-system level*, below).

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

The environment still supplies the energy source, a disequilibrium such as light, a redox pair, a gradient, or energy-rich compounds; transduction widens the range of sources the system can use but cannot create one (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*). Transduction is necessary where energy arrives in forms too weakly favorable for production to keep pace with losses, as in the minimal realization, and a net cost where the environment supplies charged carriers, as energy-rich R in a flowing pore. Microsporidia, intracellular parasites, import much of their ATP from their host;[^tsaousis2008] for them the host's transduction meets the condition, as part of their environment, and their transporters provide provisioning. The transducing machinery must itself be renewed from the energy it makes available, and each conversion loses part of the energy, so transduction ceases to suffice where the source, less those losses, cannot both renew the transducing means and drive production as fast as components are lost.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration: the charging means are produced and renewed by the network.
- Needs the charged carrier to reach a production step before it decays (a conditional relation), met by the environment (the pore holding activated R near A and B) or by individuation.
- Chemiosmotic realizations need a boundary across which a gradient holds, met by a membrane (individuation) or by mineral walls, a relation of those realizations. Transduction is unordered with respect to individuation; the grounds, and the realizations needing a boundary, are in the realization view, *Observations*.
- Unordered with respect to resource provisioning, self-regulation, environmental responsiveness, environmental protection, waste management, and dormancy. It can share pigment types with provisioning, protection, and waste management (see *Environmental protection*, below).

### Self-regulation

#### Added elaboration

Self-regulating processes adjust the system's activity in response to the levels of its own components. The elaboration is to **compensate for variation by changing activity**, whereas regeneration and the elaborations other than environmental responsiveness tolerate variation through the fixed properties of continually renewed components. A change in the surroundings can trigger self-regulation, but only after it has altered the system's internal state: the system responds to its own state, not to the outside condition itself. It meets the support condition that component levels stay within workable proportions (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*) and, combined with other elaborations, parts of further conditions by timing them (see *Combinations at the living-system level*, below).

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a small pore with few copies of A and B, fed by a supply of R that varies more than mass action and depletion absorb, so that chance fluctuations and shifts in supply drive their proportions outside the range where production keeps pace with losses. An excess of A slows its own production, for instance by binding to and inhibiting B, so that the proportions of A and B stay within workable limits as the supply of R varies. The inhibition is not a step of production: a variant of A that binds B is an added means, renewed with A. The pore contains the network, supplies R, and carries residue away.

**Developed example.** In living cells, feedback inhibition of enzymes and the regulation of gene expression by internal metabolite levels adjust production to the cell's needs. In homeoviscous adaptation, cells shift the proportion of unsaturated lipids as temperature changes, so that the membrane stays fluid enough to work;[^sinensky1974] in Bacillus subtilis the sensor, DesK, tracks the membrane's own state and switches on a desaturase.[^aguilar2001] The switch is self-regulation; the lipids it yields are a change in what regeneration produces, widening the temperature range the chemistry permits rather than holding the temperature at the components (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*).

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

Self-regulation extends the range of tolerable environmental variation but cannot compensate for conditions beyond the system's productive capacity. Because it acts only after internal levels change, it cannot respond to an outside change before that change has its effects. Regulatory components are themselves products of the network and must be renewed. Self-regulation is necessary where variation, especially of internal origin, is large relative to the number of copies of each component, beyond what the fixed properties of the production relations absorb, and a net cost where inputs are steady and the network large (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*). It ceases to suffice where proportions shift faster than the regulating interaction responds, or further than changing activity can restore, as when the supply of R falls below what production of either catalyst needs.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration.
- Unordered with respect to individuation, resource provisioning, energy transduction, and environmental responsiveness.
- Homeostasis and metabolic control are specialized applications of self-regulation, or combinations of it with other elaborations, as in thermoregulation; development and differentiation apply it to a collective (see *Candidates assessed*, below). (Metabolism itself, as the productive network, belongs to organizational regeneration.)
- Feedback that follows from the production relations alone, such as mass action, depletion of reactants, and A and B making each other, which pulls their ratio back, is unavoidable in any chemical network. Like the inherent selectivity of copying for error correction (see *Error correction*, below), it is recorded with the fixed properties through which the environment meets the condition (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*), sufficient where variation is small. Self-regulation begins with a produced and renewed interaction that production itself does not need: a dedicated regulating component, or an added binding property of a renewed component, as when excess A inhibits B (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*).

### Environmental responsiveness

#### Added elaboration

Environment-responsive processes adjust the system's activity in response to conditions in its surroundings, registered by a component whose state tracks an outside condition. The elaboration is to **adjust activity in response to conditions in the surroundings**. Unlike self-regulation, which responds to the system's own internal condition, environmental responsiveness responds to an outside condition. It can allow adjustment before that condition affects production, but earlier adjustment is a possible advantage, not a defining requirement. Without earlier adjustment, responsiveness meets what self-regulation would, by another route, including keeping component proportions workable when an outside change would shift them. With it, responsiveness meets parts of conditions that self-regulation cannot, mostly by timing other elaborations, such as protection, dormancy, uptake, and dispersal (see *Combinations at the living-system level*, below; environment view, *Observations*).

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a pore where R arrives in brief pulses, so that the rise in production that mass action gives while each pulse lasts falls short, over the cycle, of losses. The network produces a sensing component that binds R arriving in the pore. When bound, the sensor speeds up the production of A, so production increases as soon as more R arrives. Mass action alone already raises production when R rises; the sensor adds gain, raising A and B while a brief pulse lasts. Self-regulation, responding only once their levels shift, acts too late for a pulse shorter than that, and holding proportions can even damp the response. The pore contains the network; no boundary of its own is needed. Placing the sensor at the surface of the jelly instead combines responsiveness with individuation and provisioning, so that production increases as soon as R arrives rather than only after captured R has built up inside.

**Developed example.** In living cells, membrane receptors detect nutrients or harmful substances in the surroundings and switch on the corresponding uptake or protective processes. Bacterial chemotaxis extends this to moving toward or away from them.

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

Responsiveness depends on outside conditions being tracked reliably, and on the tracked condition being correlated with what matters to the system. It can allow earlier adjustment but cannot compensate for conditions beyond the system's productive capacity. Sensing components must themselves be produced and renewed. Its thresholds, where it is necessary and where the environment meets the timing so that it is a net cost, are those of timing (environment view, *Observations*, *Control meets conditions by timing*). It ceases to suffice where outside changes act faster than the tracking component can register them and switch activity, where the tracked condition no longer predicts what matters to the system, or where the means it switches cannot meet the condition even when switched on.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration.
- Unordered with respect to individuation: tracking an outside condition needs only a distinction between system and surroundings, which is drawn by production, not location (Glossary, *Environment*). R is an outside condition even within the pore, because the network does not produce it, so a sensor free in the pore needs no boundary of the network's own.
- Unordered with respect to resource provisioning, energy transduction, and self-regulation.
- Signaling between systems, sensory systems, and learning are mechanisms or specialized applications of responsiveness, not further elaborations (see *Candidates assessed*, below).
- The minimal distinction from self-regulation is what the tracking component binds, not where it is or what caused the change: a component or by-product of the system's own (self-regulation), or a condition the system does not produce (environmental responsiveness), such as R in the pore or hydrogen peroxide formed inside by outside UV. The distinction becomes consequential where outside changes act faster than internal levels shift, as with brief pulses of R or of a toxin, and where the means switched cannot be kept on at tolerable cost (environment view, *Observations*). Each also takes over a share the other cannot: self-regulation, variation of internal origin, such as fluctuation where copies are few or the system's own by-products building up, which no outside condition tracks; responsiveness, outside changes faster than internal levels shift. They are therefore distinct elaborations rather than specialized applications of one (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).

### Environmental protection

#### Added elaboration

Protecting processes reduce harm to the productive network from outside influences, such as radiation, oxidants, toxins, and parasitic components, through means that block, absorb, neutralize, sequester, or export those influences before they harm its components. The elaboration is to **limit harm from outside before it occurs**. Without it, harm is limited only by shelter in the surroundings, such as overlying water or rock blocking UV, and by regeneration replacing damaged components as fast as they are lost.

Protection reduces loss; it does not restore what is lost. Restoring a damaged component that the network can remake from raw materials is a further route of production: the network makes the working component from the damaged one rather than from R. It meets no support condition the environment could otherwise meet, so it belongs to organizational regeneration, widening what regeneration can replace. Restoration that needs information carried only by another copy, as when a damaged DNA strand is restored from its complement, is drawn the same way, by contribution: where it keeps a working component working, as in a mule or a nerve cell that will not reproduce, it belongs to organizational regeneration; where it makes transmission reliable, it is error correction. In a reproducing cell one mechanism provides both, as catalase provides protection and waste management (realization view, *Observations*).

- **From individuation:** individuation keeps the network's components in; protection keeps harmful influences out or disarms them. A membrane can do both, but each occurs without the other: a pigment free in a pore protects a network the pore contains, and a transparent jelly holds A and B together without protecting them.
- **From waste management, by source:** protection acts on outside influences, including by-products of other systems; waste management acts on by-products of the system's own activity, wherever they are (Glossary, *By-product*). Source, not location or means, draws the line. Reactive oxygen from the system's own respiration or photosynthesis, including peroxide its enzymes form accidentally from outside oxygen, is a by-product; hydrogen peroxide arriving from the surroundings, or formed inside when outside UV strikes the system's molecules, is an outside influence, as it is for environmental responsiveness (see *Environmental responsiveness*, above). The environment meets the two conditions differently: it limits outside harm by what reaches the components, through absence or shelter, and limits by-products by carrying them away faster than they form; no shelter helps against by-products, which form at the components. Mechanisms that act on a species or on heat whatever its source, such as catalase, efflux pumps, and evaporative cooling, provide both in proportion to the sources (realization view, *Observations*). Each elaboration also occurs without the other: a UV pigment or a restriction enzyme protects without managing waste, and pyrophosphatase manages waste without protecting. Because they take over different contributions from the environment, they are distinct elaborations rather than specialized applications of one (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).
- **From error correction:** protection reduces damage to heritable components before it occurs; error correction corrects errors and damage against a second copy.
- **From durable components:** a network that makes more durable variants of A or B resists damage without an added means. That is a change in what regeneration produces, not protection.

UV protection is a specialized application of protection; protection switched on by a tracked outside condition is a combination with environmental responsiveness (see *Combinations at the living-system level*, below). Holding the medium or heat at the components against the surroundings, as cuticles, fur, and compatible solutes do (see the developed examples, below), is also a specialized application: like blocking an influence, it takes over shelter, the surroundings standing between the components and a harmful exchange, inward or outward. It thereby meets a second support condition, the medium and physical range, as individuation meets several (environment view, *Observations*; Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).

#### How the system achieves it

A minimal realization needs no sensing: a renewed means with fixed properties suffices.

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a shallow pore open to sunlit water, where UV entering the pore breaks A and B, so that losses approach what regeneration can replace. The network also makes from R a pigment that absorbs UV and releases the energy as heat, so less reaches A and B. The pore holds the pigment with the network; no individuation is needed. The pigment is itself slowly broken by UV and is renewed. The surroundings still contain the network, supply R, carry residue away, and provide the rest of the shelter: the pigment reduces exposure rather than removing it. A scavenging component that reacts with an outside oxidant before it reaches A and B works the same way, but is used up, so its production must keep pace with exposure.

**Developed examples.** Cyanobacteria deposit scytonemin in their sheath, where it absorbs UV before it enters the cell;[^garciapichel1991] mycosporine-like amino acids absorb UV inside cells of cyanobacteria and algae. Animals such as corals obtain them from food or symbionts,[^shick2002] so for them a protective means made elsewhere is provisioned, and the environment meets part of the condition. The outer membrane of gram-negative bacteria excludes many toxins, jointly with individuation; crystalline surface layers (S-layers) of many bacteria and most archaea form a protective coat and molecular sieve;[^sara2000] and the coat and cortex of Bacillus spores, with proteins binding the DNA, resist radiation, heat, and chemicals.[^setlow2006] Catalase and superoxide dismutase destroy outside oxidants; the marine cyanobacterium Prochlorococcus lacks catalase and depends on neighboring bacteria to remove hydrogen peroxide from seawater, so other systems meet the condition as part of its environment.[^morris2012] Efflux pumps export antibiotics and other outside toxins,[^du2018] and restriction enzymes cut foreign DNA entering the cell while sparing the cell's own, which is marked. The biofilm matrix, polymers made by many cells, shields them from drying, antimicrobials, and grazers, while also holding cells together and retaining nutrients.[^flemming2010] Other means hold the medium or physical range at the components against the surroundings (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*): cuticles and sheaths slow loss of water, fur and blubber slow loss of heat, contractile vacuoles of freshwater protists export water entering from outside, and compatible solutes (osmoprotectants) such as glycine betaine and trehalose keep water in cells in salty surroundings. E. coli also takes up glycine betaine from its surroundings, which is provisioning of a protective means.[^csonka1989]

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

The environment still supplies materials, energy, and the medium. Protection widens the range of outside influences a system tolerates, but not the physical range its chemistry permits, and holds the medium and range at the components only as far as the surroundings allow (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*). Protective means are exposed to the influences they block and must be renewed faster than they are destroyed; scavengers are consumed in proportion to exposure. Protection costs production that could renew A and B, so it is necessary where exposure would otherwise outrun regeneration, a net cost where shelter suffices, and ceases to suffice where its means are destroyed faster than they are renewed. A barrier must still let resources in: one that excluded R would end production, the constraint individuation also faces. Means outside the system's components, such as extracellular enzymes and the biofilm matrix, protect neighbors as well, including systems that do not make them.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration: the protective means are produced and renewed by the network.
- Needs the protective means to remain between the outside influence and the components it protects (a conditional relation), met by the environment (the pore holding the pigment) or by individuation (a coating). Unordered with respect to individuation.
- Unordered with respect to resource provisioning, energy transduction, self-regulation, environmental responsiveness, and waste management. It can share mechanisms with waste management (catalase, efflux pumps). Carotenoids serve three roles: harvesting light for energy transduction is resource provisioning, dissipating excess light is protection against an outside influence, and quenching singlet oxygen made by the system's own photosynthesis is waste management (realization view, *Mechanisms in a living cell*).
- Applied to heritable components, protection meets part of heredity's condition that transmission is reliable (environment view, *Heredity*).
- A means made jointly by many systems, such as a biofilm matrix, belongs to the collective whose organization includes it; for each member, the condition it meets is met mainly by others, as part of its environment (realization view, *Observations*).

### Waste management

#### Added elaboration

Waste-managing processes prevent by-products of the system's own activity from accumulating in forms or amounts that impair continuation, by converting them to harmless forms, holding them in an inactive form (sequestration), or exporting them. The elaboration is to **keep the system's own by-products below harmful levels**. Without it, by-products are removed by dispersal, dilution, or neutralization in the surroundings, such as flowing water carrying them away.

By-products harm by binding catalysts, by driving reactions backward as they build up, or by damaging components, as reactive oxygen does. Products of a catalyst's side reactions, and products that divert production without contributing to it, are by-products in the same sense.

- **From organizational regeneration:** reusing a by-product as raw material is production, so it belongs to regeneration; it is waste management insofar as it keeps a harmful by-product below harmful levels. One mechanism can provide both, as in enzymes that turn damaged metabolites back into useful ones.[^linster2013]
- **From resource provisioning:** provisioning brings resources to where production needs them; export moves by-products away. One transporter can do both, as in the bacterial antiporter that takes in arginine while exporting the ornithine made from it.
- **From self-regulation:** slowing production as by-products build up is self-regulation, trading production for lower levels without removing anything. Increasing conversion as they build up is a combination of the two.
- **From environmental protection:** by source (see *Environmental protection*, above).
- **From energy transduction:** by-products that build up drive production backward, so removing them preserves its driving force, as pyrophosphatase does; this is waste management, since it moves no energy onto a carrier. Membrane pyrophosphatases that pump protons as they split pyrophosphate also capture its energy in a gradient, and so provide energy transduction as well.

Conversion, sequestration, and export are specialized applications of waste management, not separate elaborations: each takes over carrying away, which the surroundings do by dispersal, dilution, or neutralization, by a different route, just as protection's routes of blocking, neutralizing, sequestering, and exporting belong to one elaboration (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*). Export's need for a boundary is a relation of that route (see *Prerequisites*, below).

#### How the system achieves it

Each production reaction in the A/B network releases a small by-product, residue (realization view, *A primitive A/B network example*). At high levels, residue binds A and B and slows their catalysis. In the other accounts the surroundings carry residue away as R arrives, so it never reaches such levels.

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The network occupies a pore that exchanges with the flow only slowly, so residue builds up until production of A and B falls toward their losses. The network also makes from R a converting component that turns residue into an inert form that binds neither A nor B. The conversion is energetically favorable, so R supplies all the energy needed. The pore still contains the network, R supplies material and energy, and the inert form, harmless at the levels reached, still leaves slowly. No individuation is needed: conversion acts wherever residue is. Turning residue back into R would instead be reuse, which needs energy, since residue is what remains after R's usable energy is spent; usable energy for that step is met by a second energy-rich resource or by energy transduction.

**Developed examples.** Pyrophosphate, released whenever cells join nucleotides or amino acids, is split into phosphate by pyrophosphatase, which also keeps the joining reactions from running backward. Catalase and superoxide dismutase remove reactive oxygen made by the cell's own metabolism; in E. coli, most hydrogen peroxide forms when the cell's own enzymes react accidentally with oxygen.[^imlay2013] Most aquatic animals excrete ammonia, which the surrounding water dilutes, whereas land animals convert it at an energy cost to urea, or to uric acid, which is held as crystals until excreted; frogs switch from ammonia as tadpoles to urea as adults.[^wright1995] Cells export by-products across their membrane, such as acetate from overflow metabolism. E. coli gathers damaged, aggregated protein at its old pole, and division leaves most of it with one daughter, which grows more slowly.[^lindner2008]

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

Conversion and sequestration change the form or place of a by-product, but its final products must still leave or be held without limit. Export shifts the by-product to the surroundings, which must carry it away; it remains the system's by-product, so in a closed environment export fails, as when a network acidifies its own pore. In a culture acidifying its medium, each cell's own share of the acid is a by-product and the other cells' share an outside influence (Glossary, *By-product*). Whether waste management is needed depends on the surroundings: water dilutes ammonia, and its absence makes conversion necessary. It is necessary where the surroundings carry by-products away more slowly than they form, so that they build up to harmful levels, and a net cost where flow or dilution keeps them low. It ceases to suffice where by-products form faster than its means convert, sequester, or export them, or where its final products can neither leave nor be held, as when export fills a closed pore. Restricted transport that keeps networks separate must still let residue leave; the two are compatible because residue is small and moves as fast as R, whereas separation concerns A and B (Glossary, *Organizational separation*). Elaborations can create the need: a jelly that holds A and B, or captures R, may also hold residue that an open pore would lose. The converting means must themselves be produced and renewed, and some conversions, such as ammonia to urea, cost energy. Exported by-products reach neighbors, including the system's own offspring, as outside influences, and conversion in shared surroundings benefits systems that do not convert.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration: the means are produced and renewed by the network.
- Makes a difference only where the environment leaves a by-product at harmful levels (a conditional relation).
- Export needs a boundary across which by-products move, a conditional relation of that realization met by individuation; conversion and sequestration need none. Unordered with respect to individuation.
- Reuse of a spent by-product as raw material needs usable energy, met by a second energy-rich resource or by energy transduction (a conditional relation of that realization).
- Unordered with respect to resource provisioning, energy transduction, self-regulation, environmental responsiveness, and environmental protection.
- Removing by-products that damage heritable components, such as reactive oxygen, meets part of heredity's condition that transmission is reliable (environment view, *Heredity*).

### Heat gain

#### Added elaboration

Heat-gaining processes hold the temperature at the system's components above that of its surroundings, by heat released from the system's own energy carriers or absorbed from an outside source. The elaboration is to **keep the components warm enough for production to keep pace with losses where the surroundings are too cold**. Without it, the temperature at the components is that of the surroundings, apart from heat released by production and retained by bulk alone. It meets, for temperature, the condition that the chemistry has its medium and a physical range it permits at the components (environment view, *Organizational regeneration*).

- **From organizational regeneration:** every production step releases some heat. In a large enough system, or in well-insulating surroundings, that heat alone warms it, as microbial activity heats a compost heap, and large ectotherms hold their warmth partly by bulk. Like self-localization (see *Individuation*, above), this adds no means and is recorded with the fixed properties through which the environment meets the condition. Heat gain begins with a produced and renewed means that releases or absorbs heat and that production itself does not need where the surroundings are warm enough: a dedicated heating component, an uncoupling protein, or a dark pigment (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*).
- **From environmental protection:** protection acts on an outside influence or on exchange with the surroundings: a barrier slows loss of water or heat, and compatible solutes keep water from being drawn out. Heat gain supplies a condition that no material carries: in uniformly cold surroundings no influence acts on the components that could be blocked or neutralized. Insulation is protection; it retains heat, whether released by production or by heat gain.
- **From waste management:** waste management keeps the system's own by-products below harmful levels, whereas heat gain uses heat to hold the components above a lower limit. Shedding the system's own excess heat, as by evaporation, is waste management; shedding heat arriving from the sun is protection, and one mechanism can provide both. The line between them, drawn by source, concerns harm, so it does not apply to heat gain.
- **From resource provisioning and energy transduction:** both concern what production consumes: provisioning where and when a resource is available, transduction the form of energy. Heat at the components is consumed by no production step; it sets their rates. Heat gain draws on the energy source as production does.
- **From a chemistry tolerating a wider range:** making components that work at lower temperatures, as in homeoviscous adaptation, or adding solutes that stabilize them, as trimethylamine N-oxide stabilizes the proteins of deep-sea fish against pressure,[^yancey2014] is a change in what regeneration produces: it changes the range the condition requires rather than meeting it (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*; see *Self-regulation*, above). Heat gain changes the temperature at the components instead.

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network fills a large, closed pore in poorly conducting rock, fed by a slow seep of cool water carrying R. At that temperature A and B catalyze too slowly for production to keep pace with losses, which are by outflow and so are not slowed by cold. The network also makes from R a heating component that catalyzes the favorable breakdown of R into residue, releasing its energy as heat without making A or B. The rock retains the heat, so the water in the pore warms until heat lost through the rock matches heat released, within the range where production keeps pace. The pore holds the network, supplies R in excess of what production needs, retains the heat, and lets residue leave with the outflow; no individuation is needed. Replacing the heating component with a warm pore leaves regeneration unchanged, with the environment meeting the condition.

**Developed examples.** Brown fat in mammals releases heat through an uncoupling protein, which lets protons cross the mitochondrial membrane without making ATP; shivering releases heat from muscle contraction.[^lowell2000] Bumblebees warm their flight muscles by shivering before flight. Thermogenic plants such as the sacred lotus release heat through an alternative oxidase and hold their flowers near 30 to 35 °C while the air varies more widely.[^seymour1996] Swordfish warm their eyes and brain with a heater organ, modified eye muscle that releases heat without contracting.[^carey1982] Dark pigments absorbing sunlight warm many ectotherms above the air. A single cell can do none of this: heat leaves so small a volume so fast that a cell's metabolism raises its temperature by a minute fraction of a degree.[^baffou2014]

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

The environment still supplies the energy source and the medium. Heat gain holds the components above the temperature of the surroundings only by a difference its heat output can sustain against heat loss, so it ceases to suffice where the surroundings are colder than that difference allows, and it widens the range of surroundings a system tolerates without making any surroundings suitable (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*). Heat loss grows with surface relative to volume, so heat gain makes a difference only in large systems, or where the surroundings or insulation retain the heat. It costs energy that could drive production, so it is net beneficial only where cold slows production more than it slows losses, and a net cost elsewhere; it is necessary where cold slows production below losses, as in the minimal realization. The heating means must themselves be produced and renewed. Overshooting the range harms as cold does, so heat gain is usually combined with control (see *Combinations at the living-system level*, below).

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration: the heating means are produced and renewed by the network.
- Needs the released heat to be retained near the components (a conditional relation), met by the environment (a large pore in poorly conducting rock) or by individuation with size, and in part by protection (insulation). Unordered with respect to individuation.
- Needs energy beyond what production consumes (a conditional relation), met by the environment or by resource provisioning and energy transduction.
- Unordered with respect to resource provisioning, energy transduction, self-regulation, environmental responsiveness, environmental protection, waste management, and dormancy.

### Dormancy

#### Added elaboration

Dormant processes preserve the system's organization through an interruption of its support conditions, by means that slow the loss of its components while regeneration is suspended, so that regeneration can resume when conditions return. The elaboration is to **outlast an interruption that regeneration cannot**. Without it, a network whose support conditions fail, as when its pore dries, its supply stops, or its temperature leaves the range its chemistry permits, goes on losing components with nothing replacing them, and survives only interruptions shorter than that loss allows. While dormant, the system continues as a rock does, by the durability of its parts; the durability differs in being produced by the system and in preserving an organization that regenerates again (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*). Other elaborations keep production going under limiting conditions; dormancy addresses intervals in which production cannot go on at all, including those set by conditions only the environment can meet (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*).

- **From environmental protection:** protection blocks or neutralizes outside influences while the network operates; dormancy slows all loss while it does not, including the spontaneous decay of its own components, which no barrier prevents. Reduced susceptibility that follows from inactivity alone, as when antibiotics acting on growth spare inactive cells, is a consequence of suspension, not a protective means.
- **From self-regulation and slowed regeneration:** a system that slows its activity but still makes good most of what it loses is regenerating, whether the slowing follows from reduced supply or from self-regulation, a produced interaction that changes activity. Regeneration is suspended where renewal makes good less than half of what is lost, a graded threshold (Basis, *Suspended regeneration*). Dormancy is drawn not by that threshold but by its means: a produced and renewed means that slows the loss of components and that production itself does not need where the environment meets the condition (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*). Slower loss that follows from inactivity alone adds no means; like the feedback that follows from the production relations (see *Self-regulation*, above), it is recorded with the fixed properties through which the environment meets the condition. A suspended system without such a means is interrupted, not dormant.
- **From resource provisioning:** storage keeps production going through a shortage and ends when stores run out; dormancy suspends the need for supply, and bridges interruptions, such as drying, that no store can. Reserves used to resume, as in seeds, combine the two.
- **From durable components:** making more durable variants of A or B is a change in what regeneration produces, not dormancy (as for protection, above).

#### How the system achieves it

A minimal realization needs no sensing: a means with fixed properties, made while conditions are favorable, suffices.

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a shallow pore that dries out from time to time for longer than A and B survive when dry. The network also makes from R a glassy component that, as the pore dries, sets around A and B and slows their decay many-fold. When water returns, the glass dissolves, and A and B, still together in the pore, resume making each other and the glassy component, which is renewed as the flow carries it away. The pore holds A and B through the dry interval, R arrives with the water, and residue leaves with the flow; the water must return before the glassed A and B are lost. No individuation is needed.

**Developed examples.** The Bacillus endospore holds its genome in a dehydrated core with calcium dipicolinate, where metabolism is undetectable, and can resume after years; its coat and DNA-binding proteins add protection, and receptors sensing nutrients trigger resumption.[^setlow2006] Tardigrades drying slowly contract into a "tun", in which disordered proteins form a glass, and resume within hours of rehydration after years;[^boothby2017] Keilin called such states latent life, or cryptobiosis.[^keilin1959] Orthodox seeds dry to a glassy state and resume on wetting; seed biologists call this state quiescence, reserving "seed dormancy" for withholding germination under favorable conditions until a cue such as winter cold.[^baskin2004] A hydrated seed withholding germination respires and repairs, so it is regenerating, and the withholding is environmental responsiveness timing growth, not dormancy. Bacterial persister cells are not dormant in this sense: slowed or arrested by a switch that responds to the levels of the cell's own components, such as a toxin outweighing its antitoxin, they make no preserving means and tolerate antibiotics through inactivity, so the switch is self-regulation.[^balaban2004] Microbial ecologists use "dormancy" more broadly, for any reversible state of low metabolic activity, including slowed regeneration.[^lennon2011] Lichens dry and resume as a collective, both partners suspended together (Basis, *Counting and continuity*).

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

The environment must still keep the preserved components together and undestroyed, and must bring conditions back within their preserved lifetime; dormancy lengthens the interruption a system survives but cannot bring conditions back (environment view, *Organizational regeneration* and *Conditions only the environment can meet*). Preserved components still decay slowly, so the viability of spores and seeds declines with time, and damage accumulated without repair is made good on resumption by regeneration, or for heritable components by error correction. The preserving means cost production, and time spent dormant is time not producing or reproducing, so dormancy is a net cost where interruptions end well within what unpreserved components survive, necessary where they exceed it, and ceases to suffice where they exceed what preserved components survive. Entering dormancy can itself need conditions, as tardigrades survive slow drying but not fast; resuming needs regeneration's support conditions at once, or reserves. At the lineage level, offspring resuming at different times spread the lineage across times (*Combinations at the lineage level*, below); at the heredity level, dormant stages carry variants forward from earlier times,[^lennon2011] meeting reproduction's condition of spreading for the systems that carry them rather than a further condition of heredity (environment view, *Heredity*).

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates organizational regeneration: the preserving means are produced by the network, and resumption is regeneration.
- Needs the preserved components to stay together through the interruption (a conditional relation), met by the environment (the pore) or by individuation (spore, tun).
- Needs every essential component to survive the interruption in at least one copy, which chance meets where copies are many.
- Unordered with respect to individuation, resource provisioning, energy transduction, self-regulation, environmental responsiveness, environmental protection, and waste management. Entering or leaving dormancy on a cue is a combination (see *Combinations at the living-system level*, below).

### Combinations at the living-system level

- **Responsive protection:** environmental responsiveness combined with environmental protection, switching on protective means when a tracked outside condition rises, as when UV-A induces scytonemin synthesis in cyanobacteria.[^garciapichel1991] Moving away from a harmful substance (negative chemotaxis) combines responsiveness with motility; motility then provides protection, as it provides provisioning when it moves the cell toward resources. Moving between warmer and cooler sites (behavioral thermoregulation) is the same combination: the environment meets the condition at the site reached.
- **Thermoregulation:** heat gain combined with environmental protection (insulation, and barriers to heat from outside), waste management (shedding the system's own heat), and self-regulation or environmental responsiveness, holding the temperature at the components within a range. Control by a component tracking heat the system itself holds, such as core temperature in a mammal, is self-regulation; control by one tracking the temperature of the surroundings is responsiveness.
- **Regulated waste management:** self-regulation combined with waste management, increasing conversion or export as by-products build up.
- **Energy storage:** resource provisioning (storage) combined with energy transduction, holding energy in a form charged and later discharged by transduction, as in glycogen or polyphosphate converted to ATP. Holding a supplied energy-rich resource, as jelly holding energy-rich R, is provisioning alone.
- **Responsive dormancy:** environmental responsiveness combined with dormancy, entering or leaving it on a tracked outside condition, as when tardigrades form a tun as they dry or spores resume on sensing nutrients. Triggered by the system's own state, as when starvation triggers sporulation, dormancy is combined with self-regulation instead.

## Elaborations of the lineage

These elaborations meet support conditions of reproduction (see the environment view, *Reproduction*). They act on new units, which exist only where reproduction occurs.

Coordinated division, an earlier candidate, combined three contributions that can occur independently: separating new units, which the environment or individuation can provide; partitioning components between them; and timing division to growth, a combination with self-regulation. Only partitioning is retained as an elaboration. Provisioning offspring, another earlier candidate, is a combination (see *Combinations at the lineage level*, below).

### Partitioning

#### Added elaboration

Partitioning processes distribute a system's components between new units so that each receives a complete working set. The elaboration is to **make the completeness of new units reliable rather than a matter of chance**. Without it, completeness depends on chance, which suffices when each new unit receives many copies of every component: if fragments hold on average five copies each of A and B, about 99 percent contain both. Chance fails when an essential component has few copies; a component present in only two copies at division leaves one of two new units without it half the time.

Partitioning concerns which components each new unit receives. Separating the units is met by the environment or by individuation, and timing division to growth is a combination with self-regulation (see *Combinations at the lineage level*, below).

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a pore field whose channels are so narrow that founding sets are small, carrying few copies of A and B, so that chance leaves too few complete new networks for the lineage to replace its losses. The network makes, from R, a linking component that binds A to B in pairs. Any founding set containing a pair, whether diffusing into a vacant pore or held in a jelly fragment, contains a complete working set however small it is, and can go on to make more of the linking component. The pore geometry or disturbance still meets separation, and the surroundings still supply room, R, and energy; no individuation is needed.

**Developed example.** In bacteria, low-copy plasmids carry partition machinery (such as ParMRC and ParABS) that moves one copy into each daughter cell, while high-copy plasmids segregate at random. The Min system places the division ring at midcell; cells lacking it produce small cells without DNA. Joining genes into one chromosome works like the linking component: genes on one molecule are transmitted together.[^maynardsmith1993]

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

Chance assortment, as in fatty-acid vesicles dividing under shear[^zhu2009] and in the stochastic corrector,[^szathmary1987] costs a fraction of incomplete new units, whose material is lost to the lineage. The surroundings can also improve completeness: bacterial L-forms, which divide without division machinery, segregate their chromosomes much more efficiently when confined to narrow channels.[^wu2020] The minimal cell JCVI-syn3.0[^hutchison2016] divides into cells of widely varying size and shape, and restoring seven genes removed in its design returns regular division.[^pelletier2021] Partitioning reduces the loss of incomplete units and allows essential components present in a single copy, as in template heredity. It also reduces the variation between new units that chance assortment produces, on which selection between units can act (the stochastic corrector). Partitioning is necessary where chance leaves too few complete new units for the lineage to replace its losses: with an essential component present in a single copy, binary division by chance gives exactly one complete unit per division, so any further loss makes the lineage decline. It is a net cost where every essential component has many copies, and it ceases to suffice where growth does not supply a copy of each for every new unit.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates reproduction: it acts on the formation of new units.
- Needs at least one copy of each essential component for each new unit, which growth supplies (environment view, *Reproduction*).
- Makes a difference only where some essential component has few copies relative to the number of new units (a conditional relation).
- Unordered with respect to individuation: the linking component works in the pore field without a produced boundary.
- Applied to heritable components, partitioning also meets part of heredity's condition that transmission is reliable, carrying a variant into each new unit (see error correction, below).

### Dispersal

#### Added elaboration

Dispersal processes carry new units to sites where they can continue. The elaboration is to **extend the reach of new units beyond what transport in the surroundings provides**. Without it, new units reach only vacant sites within reach of diffusion or currents. Its effect has no counterpart in a single system: more sites can be occupied, and the lineage is spread across sites, so that one local disruption need not end it.

Dispersal differs from resource provisioning, which makes resources available to a system, whereas dispersal brings a new unit to a site. It differs from environmental responsiveness, since passive dispersal tracks no outside condition.

Passive and active dispersal are specialized applications of one elaboration: both take over reach beyond what transport in the surroundings provides, passive means by extending that transport, as release or wings do, and active means by replacing it, as motility does. They differ from heat gain and insulation, which take over different things the surroundings do, warmth and shelter from loss of heat; here the surroundings do one thing, transport (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies a pore field in which the vacant pores within reach of diffusion are already occupied, so that the lineage, founding new networks only there, replaces its losses too slowly. A and B are adsorbed on fine mineral grains in the pore, which keep a founding set together. The network makes a releasing component that loosens grains carrying A and B from the pore wall, so that the flow carries them to vacant pores further downstream. The surroundings supply the flow, the vacant pores, and R at the destination; the distance travelled meets separation. In a combined variant with individuation, a slippery coating component on jelly fragments keeps them from sticking to pore walls.

**Developed example.** Passive means use transport in the surroundings, as in winged and plumed seeds, small light spores, and enzymes that release cells from a biofilm. Active means use the system's own energy, as in flagellar motility. In Caulobacter, division produces a stalked cell that stays attached and a motile daughter that disperses.

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

The surroundings must still contain vacant sites where new units can continue; dispersal extends reach but cannot create room. Passive dispersal depends on the transport available, and active dispersal costs the system's own production. In a pore field, faster continuous exchange between pores would carry founders further but weaken organizational separation; moving founding sets as units avoids this. Dispersal is necessary where vacant sites within reach of transport in the surroundings are used up, or where disruptions are correlated across the sites within reach, and a net cost where that transport reaches vacant, scattered sites; it ceases to suffice where no vacant sites remain.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates reproduction: it acts on new units.
- Needs the founding set to stay together in transit, a conditional relation met by the environment (adsorption on mineral grains) or by individuation (jelly fragments, cells).
- Active dispersal needs usable energy beyond what production consumes (a conditional relation of that realization), met by the environment or by resource provisioning and energy transduction; passive dispersal needs transport in the surroundings.
- Unordered with respect to resource provisioning: a seed can be dispersed passively without provisioning, and a cell can provision itself without dispersal. Motility is a mechanism that can provide both (realization view).
- Unordered with respect to partitioning: a linking component works without release, and released grains carry founding sets complete by chance where copies are many.
- Spreading the lineage across sites meets a support condition of reproduction distinct from room: that the lineage's systems are not all lost to one disruption (environment view, *Reproduction*).

### Combinations at the lineage level

Each links a contribution of a living-system elaboration to reproduction, directly or through dispersal (Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations*).

- **Provisioning offspring:** resource provisioning (storage) combined with reproduction, supplying new units with reserves for their establishment, as in yolk and seed reserves. It moves the growth that establishment needs from the new unit to its parent, as in eggs whose first divisions proceed without growth. In the A/B network, jelly that captures R (resource provisioning) and breaks into fragments (reproduction) gives each fragment its own captured R, with no further mechanism, so it is not a separate elaboration. Budding is not provisioning offspring: the bud is built from the parent's working components, not from reserves (see Base Capabilities, *Reproduction*). Provisioning can continue after birth, as in lactation, without making separation partial: milk supplies mainly materials that the offspring's own production transforms, so the parent meets a support condition as part of the offspring's environment. The same holds in pregnancy, since separation is drawn by production, not location (Glossary, *Organizational separation*). Maternal antibodies are a minor exception: produced components that serve the offspring's protection rather than its regeneration.
- **Division timed to growth:** self-regulation combined with reproduction, dividing once the system's own components suffice for each new unit, as in the bacterial "adder".[^taheriaraghi2015]
- **Directed dispersal:** environmental responsiveness combined with dispersal, as in chemotaxis toward new sites.
- **Dormant dispersal:** dormancy combined with dispersal, so that new units outlast a transit longer than their unpreserved components survive, extending the reach of dispersal, as in fungal spores and dry seeds. Protective coats, where present, are environmental protection provided by the same mechanism rather than a further part, since dormant dispersal occurs without them (realization view, *Mechanisms in a living cell*).
- **Bet-hedging:** dormancy combined with reproduction, in which offspring resume at different times, so that the lineage is spread across times, as in the seed banks of annual plants.[^cohen1966] Bacteria switching at random into the persister state spread the lineage across times in the same way, but reach inactivity by self-regulation without a preserving means, so there the combination is self-regulation with reproduction.[^balaban2004] Either meets the support condition that the lineage's systems are not all lost to one disruption (environment view, *Reproduction*).
- **Segregating damage:** waste management (sequestration) combined with reproduction, gathering damaged components so that division leaves them mainly with one new unit, as with protein aggregates in E. coli.[^lindner2008]
- **Transgenerational induction:** environmental responsiveness combined with reproduction, in which a condition tracked by the parent switches means in new units, so that they meet it before tracking it themselves, as when Daphnia exposed to cues from predators produce young with defensive helmets.[^agrawal1999] Where the switched means is protection, as here, it is responsive protection carried into new units. In CRISPR immunity, a cell records a segment of an infecting phage's DNA in its genome, and daughters inherit the record with the protection it switches on.[^barrangou2007] The carried state, such as a maternal signal, a chromatin mark, or a recorded segment, is the state of the responsiveness means rather than a further means.

## Elaborations of the evolving lineage

These elaborations meet support conditions of heredity (see the environment view, *Heredity*). They are error correction and recombination; sexual reproduction is a combination (see *Combinations at the evolving lineage level*, below).

Copying fidelity, an earlier candidate, listed template copying among its means. Template copying is a means of inheritance, by which heredity itself occurs (Glossary, *Heredity*), so the elaboration is restated as error correction.

### Error correction

#### Added elaboration

Error-correcting processes detect errors in heritable components and correct them against a second copy of the same information. The elaboration is to **keep transmission errors below what the lineage's heritable information can tolerate**. Without it, reliability depends on conditions that limit errors, such as moderate temperature and low radiation, and on the inherent selectivity of the chemistry: in enzyme-free template copying, a mismatch slows further copying, so that error-laden copies fall behind.[^rajamani2010][^leu2013] The amount of heritable information that can persist is limited by the error threshold (Glossary): enzyme-free copying, with errors of a few percent per unit, can maintain sequences of only tens of units. A produced copying catalyst raises the selectivity of the copying step without correcting errors, and its selectivity belongs to the means of inheritance: polymerases without a proofreading exonuclease err roughly once in 10^4 to 10^5 units, which limits RNA viruses lacking one to genomes below about 20,000 bases (see *How the system achieves it*, below).

Transmission can fail in three ways. A variant may fail to reach a new unit, which partitioning meets; heritable components may be damaged between transmissions, which environmental protection and waste management reduce; and errors may arise when the variant is reproduced. Error correction meets the last, and also repairs damage where a second copy is available. The same repair in a system that will not reproduce keeps a working component working, which is organizational regeneration (see *Environmental protection*, above). Discrimination by the copying step itself, as when a polymerase favors correct pairs, belongs to the means of inheritance (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*); error correction is a further means that detects and removes errors.

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** In a template variant of the A/B network, following Lincoln and Joyce,[^lincoln2009] A and B are complementary strands, and each, as template and catalyst, joins two pieces made from R into the other. The strands are longer than the selectivity of the copying step sustains, so that the lineage's variant is lost to errors faster than selection maintains it. The network also makes an editing component that pulls pieces held mismatched on a template apart before they are joined; correctly paired pieces bind more tightly and escape it. The surroundings still meet mild conditions that limit damage, the pores keep lineages separate, and R supplies the pieces.

In the compositional A/B network there is no second copy to check against, so error correction cannot occur. Reliability there comes from feedback, such as the variant of A inhibiting the formation of A from R, which holds the network in the variant's composition. This is self-regulation meeting a support condition of heredity, not error correction.

**Developed example.** In living cells, the proofreading exonuclease of DNA polymerase and mismatch repair, added to the polymerase's own selectivity, reduce errors to roughly one per billion bases copied; kinetic proofreading[^hopfield1974] shows how such accuracy can exceed what binding energy alone allows. Damage repair restores a damaged strand from its complement, and Deinococcus repairs severe damage using its several genome copies. Across DNA-based microbes, mutations per genome per copying are roughly constant, so the error rate per base falls as genomes grow, as the error threshold implies.[^drake1991] Viruses, whose evolvability is that of components rather than heredity (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*), show the same physics: RNA viruses lacking a proofreading exonuclease have genomes below about 20,000 bases, while coronaviruses and other nidoviruses carrying one (nsp14) reach about 41,000;[^saberi2018] coronaviruses lacking its activity mutate many times faster.[^smith2013]

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

Error correction cannot remove variation: imperfect transmission is inherent, and the correcting means are themselves made with errors. The useful level of accuracy depends on conditions; mutator strains of bacteria, lacking mismatch repair, can spread under stress. Correction extends the conditions under which, and the amount of heritable information with which, a lineage can carry its variants, but not without limit. The correcting means must themselves be produced and renewed, which costs production and slows copying. Error correction is necessary where the lineage's heritable information exceeds what the selectivity of the copying step, enzyme-free or by a produced catalyst, and mild conditions sustain, and a net cost where the information is short enough and conditions mild enough for them to suffice; it ceases to suffice where the information exceeds about one over the error rate per unit that remains after correction.

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates heredity: it acts on the transmission of a particular organization.
- Needs a second copy of the heritable information to correct against, such as a complementary strand or additional genome copies. This holds for template inheritance and is recorded with those realizations (realization view); compositional inheritance offers no such copy.
- Heritable information beyond what the selectivity of the copying step sustains needs error correction (a conditional physical relation): the error threshold limits the information in a transmitted set to about one over the copying step's error rate per unit, as the genome sizes above illustrate. Dividing the information among several copied units does not remove the limit, and redundancy with selection between compartments, as in the stochastic corrector,[^szathmary1987] widens the range only a little. Unlimited heredity does not need so much (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*); open-ended evolution does, beyond a stated range (see *Extended endpoint*, below).

### Recombination

#### Added elaboration

Recombining processes bring heritable variants from different lineages together in one system. The elaboration is to **combine variants that arose in separate lineages**. Without it, a lineage's variants come from transmission errors in its own line and from whatever exchange through the surroundings brings, and a beneficial variant arising in one lineage joins one arising in another only by that exchange. Recombination adds new combinations of existing variants, not new variants.

Recombination differs from a separation that merely leaks. Exchange through the surroundings, such as slow diffusion between pores, moves all components alike; it is the environment's share of combinations under heredity's condition that variation arises (environment view, *Heredity*), which recombination takes over. With recombination, the system's own means move heritable components occasionally and in small sets, so organizational separation holds and vertical transmission still outweighs horizontal transfer (environment view, *Heredity*). It differs from dispersal in destination: the carried set joins an existing system rather than founding one, and need not be complete.

#### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The compositional A/B network occupies a small field of pores linked so narrowly that diffusion carries too few heritable components between them to bring back compositions that chance has lost. This shortfall in the environment's share of combinations, under heredity's condition that variation arises, is the one departure from the starting environment (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*). Side reactions make harmful variants of A that the network regenerates with its own; chance loses the networks carrying the fewest, and none regains them by its own transmission (Muller's ratchet; see *Environmental dependencies and limits*, below), so the lineage's variants degrade until it fails. The releasing component of dispersal loosens small mineral grains carrying a few adsorbed catalysts, including any variant of A. When the flow carries them into an occupied pore downstream, they join the resident network, which regenerates them alongside its own, so that catalysts from networks carrying fewer harmful variants reach those carrying more, and new networks founded from them can again carry the less error-laden compositions that chance lost. The pores still keep lineages separate, the flow supplies transport, and R supplies production. In a large field, the same means only combines variants that arose in separate lineages, which is net beneficial, never necessary.

**Developed example.** Bacteria take up DNA from the surroundings (transformation) or receive it through a cell-to-cell bridge (conjugation), and integrate it by homologous recombination. RNA viruses recombine when copying switches from one template to another, the mechanism there acting on components rather than on lineages of living systems (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). In eukaryotes, meiosis and fertilization recombine the heritable components of two parents at reproduction (see *Combinations at the evolving lineage level*, below).

#### Environmental dependencies and limits

Recombination needs lineages carrying different variants and contact between them, met by the surroundings (flow, diffusion), by dispersal, or by the fusion of individuated systems. In finite populations without it, chance loss of the least error-laden lineages cannot be reversed (Muller's ratchet); recombination restores error-free combinations, so it meets in part heredity's condition that transmission is reliable.[^felsenstein1974] This is the only route by which recombination is necessary for continuation: in small populations whose transmission errors are harmful enough that the ratchet degrades the lineage's variants faster than selection removes them, until the lineage fails.[^lynch1993] In large populations, or where few errors are harmful, the ratchet turns slowly and recombination is not necessary. Combining variants that arose in separate lineages is never necessary for continuation, since carrying particular variants forward needs no new combinations and the environment always meets the condition that variation arises; it can only be net beneficial, to evolvability (see *Extended endpoint*, below). It also breaks up favorable combinations, and its means must be produced and renewed, so at its level it is a net cost where the ratchet turns slowly, and it ceases to suffice where harmful errors arise faster than recombination and selection together restore the less error-laden combinations. Exchange beyond the threshold of vertical transmission dissolves lineages, so that lineages of systems carry no heredity and evolvability attaches to the exchanged components (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*).

#### Prerequisites

- Elaborates heredity: it combines variants carried by separate lineages, which presupposes heredity.
- Needs heritable components that stay intact when moved, such as single catalysts in compositional inheritance or segments in template inheritance (a conditional relation).
- Unordered with respect to error correction: either can occur without the other, though in cells homologous recombination is a mechanism that provides both (realization view).
- Unordered with respect to dispersal, which can provide contact between lineages; in the A/B network one releasing component provides both.

### Combinations at the evolving lineage level

- **Sexual reproduction:** recombination combined with reproduction, in which a new unit is formed from heritable components of two parents, as in meiosis and fertilization. Both parents are vertical sources, so it meets heredity's condition that heritable components pass mainly from parent to offspring. In a combined variant of the A/B network, a sticky surface component makes jelly fragments from different lineages fuse on contact; when the fused blob later breaks, its fragments combine variants from both.

## Candidates assessed

Candidates for further elaborations are assessed by the procedure in Method, *Completeness of the elaborations*. None assessed so far is a missing elaboration; each sorts as follows.

- **Signaling between systems:** in quorum sensing, members release a signal and respond to its level. For a member, the signal from other members is a condition it does not produce, so tracking it is environmental responsiveness; its own share is self-regulation, the two in proportion to the sources, as for catalase (realization view, *Observations*). For their collective, the signal is its own component, so tracking it is the collective's self-regulation, timing collective means such as matrix and extracellular enzymes.[^waters2005] It takes over the timing these elaborations take over, so it is a mechanism, and what it switches are combinations already recorded.
- **Development and differentiation:** differentiation is a collective's self-regulation and responsiveness changing what members produce, keeping the proportions of member types workable, which is that support condition applied to the collective. Development is the growth a collective founded from one member needs after its founding (environment view, *Reproduction*), timed by that regulation. The founding member is a complete working set, since its production regrows the rest, as a jelly fragment containing A and B regrows its jelly. Neither takes over anything new.
- **Division of labor:** member types provide different elaborations or base capabilities of their collective, as heterocysts fix nitrogen for a cyanobacterial filament, and as somatic cells move a Volvox colony while germ cells reproduce it. This is several mechanisms jointly providing the collective's contributions (realization view, *Observations*), and it deepens integration (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*).
- **Programmed cell death:** a system's own death is no elaboration of its regeneration, since it is not net beneficial to the system's balance. In a collective, removing damaged members is waste management for the collective, by source (Glossary, *By-product*), and members that die forming a stalk are means of dispersal for the others. Without a collective, an infected bacterium that kills itself before a phage completes its cycle (abortive infection) takes over each neighbor's shelter from the phage, so it is a specialized application of environmental protection, switched by responsiveness.[^lopatina2020] It meets in part reproduction's condition that the lineage's systems are not all lost to one disruption (environment view, *Reproduction*), and persists only where its beneficiaries are mainly of the same lineage, that is, where benefits stay with the variant that produces them (environment view, *Heredity*).
- **Motility:** a mechanism providing dispersal, resource provisioning, environmental protection, and, by reaching cooler or warmer sites, the physical range at the components (see *Combinations at the living-system level*, above, and the realization view).
- **Regulated or stress-induced mutation:** polymerases that copy past damage let copying finish, and the mutations they make are by-products of that.[^galhardo2007] A means raising variation as such would add new variants, which the imperfection inherent in transmission always supplies, so there is nothing for it to take over (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*). Recombination differs: it takes over exchange between lineages, which the surroundings can lack (environment view, *Heredity*). A variation rate above what that supplies can be net beneficial to open-ended evolution, never necessary (see *Extended endpoint*, below). Variation set by a tracked condition and carried into new units, as in CRISPR immunity, is transgenerational induction (see *Combinations at the lineage level*, above).
- **Epigenetic inheritance:** a stable state transmitted to offspring, such as a chromatin mark or a self-sustaining metabolic state, is a means of inheritance, as composition is (Glossary, *Heredity*), so it is a mechanism of heredity.[^jablonka2009] A state set by a condition the parent tracked and carried into offspring is transgenerational induction (see *Combinations at the lineage level*, above).
- **Immunity:** excluding foreign components is environmental protection, and switching that exclusion on when they are detected is responsive protection. Memory, and clonal selection among a body's lymphocytes, improve the timing and specificity of that response; timing is not a support condition (environment view, *Observations*), so they are mechanisms. CRISPR immunity is transgenerational induction (see *Combinations at the lineage level*, above).
- **Learning and memory:** past tracked conditions change how responsiveness maps conditions to activity, and memory is a lasting state of the tracking means. The takeover is that of responsiveness, so learning is a specialized application of it. Culture, learned states passing between systems, is the evolvability of components, or, for collectives that reproduce, a possible means of collective heredity (Basis, *Levels and organizations*).

Several candidates arise only in collectives: division of labor, differentiation and development, members dying for others, clonal selection, and policing of cheating members. Policing is waste management where the cheater arises within the collective and environmental protection where it is recruited from outside, and it meets in part collective heredity's condition that benefits stay with the members that produce them. Each is an existing elaboration or combination applied to the collective's organization, assessed for that organization (Glossary, *Organizational separation*); members, including by their death or reproduction, serve as its mechanisms (realization view, *Observations*).

## Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution

The study examines elaborations that support regeneration under different conditions, reproduction across systems, and heredity across generations. Open-ended evolution is a possible extended endpoint: an outcome, judged by continued production of new heritable mechanisms (Glossary, *Open-ended evolution*), not a base capability or elaboration. In the terms of the literature, the criterion asks for expansive novelty; new variants of existing mechanisms alone, exploratory novelty, do not meet it, and new collective individuals, which the literature calls new levels, give transformational novelty.[^taylor2016][^banzhaf2016] This section is the study's one home for the conditions of open-ended evolution. They concern evolving lineages and are not imposed on every living individual.

Because an outcome is graded over an interval, its conditions are stated as necessary for or net beneficial to it, for a stated environment and interval, each with its ground and scope, rather than as dependencies (Method, *Dependency criterion*).

**Necessary for open-ended evolution**

- **Regeneration, reproduction, and heredity** (definitional, unconditional): new heritable mechanisms persist only in an evolving lineage, which presupposes the dependency chain (dependency view).
- **Unlimited heredity** (definitional, unconditional): a lineage with few possible heritable variants exhausts them, and persistent novelty stops. A template of modest length meets it (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*).
- **Heritable information that can specify new mechanisms** (definitional, unconditional): novelty is counted in mechanisms, so where the mechanisms a lineage's heritable information can specify form a set the interval exhausts, novelty stops, however many variants remain. Added information does not by itself meet this, since repeats and new variants of existing mechanisms add information without adding mechanisms. Every known case meets it by modular inheritance specifying components whose units combine non-additively, so that added information can specify components with new effects, as sequences of amino acids fold into new catalysts. The range of mechanisms also depends on what there is to be met or entered (*The environment's share*, below).
- **Heritable information that can be added to** (physical, unconditional): the condition above cannot be met for long by a fixed store. Unlimited heredity is a count at one time, and a fixed store meets it, but a fixed store specifies only a bounded set of mechanisms, each needing heritable information to specify it, and a population of the order of Earth's prokaryotes would explore a fixed space of 60 units within geological time. Information is added by lengthening, by duplication, or by added heritable components.
- **Error correction, beyond a stated range** (physical, conditional): error correction is necessary once the heritable information in a transmitted set exceeds about one over the copying step's error rate per unit, which is a few tens to about a hundred units for enzyme-free copying and about 10^4 to 10^5 for a produced copying catalyst without correction (*Error correction*, *Prerequisites*). Because the information must keep growing, any fixed selectivity of the copying step is eventually exceeded, so sustained open-ended evolution needs error correction; error correction raises the limit rather than removing it. In small populations, recombination's share of reliable transmission, against Muller's ratchet, carries over (*Recombination*).

**Net beneficial, never necessary**

- **Recombination:** combining variants that arose in separate lineages, including new mechanisms passing between lineages by horizontal transfer, raises the rate of novelty (*Recombination*).
- **Raised variation rates:** a produced means raising variation can raise the rate of novelty where environments change, as mutator strains spread under stress, but variation must stay below the error threshold. It is never necessary, since the imperfection inherent in transmission always supplies new variants (*Candidates assessed*).
- **Dormant stages carrying variants:** stores of variants from earlier times slow their loss through failure of carrier systems (*Dormancy*; environment view, *Heredity*).
- **Collective heredity:** new collective individuals, which reproduce and carry collective variants, give new continuants, the strongest novelty, and widen the range of mechanisms, since members compose collective mechanisms; they apply the dependency chain to a collective's organization rather than adding a level (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems* and *Levels and organizations*). Cultural novelty counts toward the criterion only where culture is collective heredity; otherwise it is the evolvability of components, which can supply the changing environment below. The criterion does not require them.

**The environment's share**

Necessary (physical, unconditional): new conditions or environments keep becoming available to the lineages over the interval, by change in time, by variety not yet reached, or by other lineages, each part of the others' environment. A new mechanism persists only where it is net beneficial (Basis, *Logic of elaboration*). Within a fixed set of conditions, what any mechanism can add to the balance is limited by the parts only the environment can meet (environment view, *Conditions only the environment can meet*), so successive improvements give diminishing returns and the rate of novelty declines, as fitness gains slow without ceasing in Escherichia coli propagated for tens of thousands of generations in a constant environment.[^wiser2013] Change that recurs, as when hosts and parasites cycle among a few strategies, keeps existing mechanisms net beneficial in turn but makes no new one so, and does not meet the share. Other lineages supply new conditions only as far as their own mechanisms are new, so where they supply the whole share it rests on the condition that heritable information can specify new mechanisms, holding in those lineages, in a world where new components have new effects. How much variety a stated interval needs grows with the interval.

**Sufficiency**

The conditions above are sufficient in the sense set out in Method, *Testing the conditions of an outcome*: no case assessed meets every necessary condition throughout a stated interval while its production of new heritable mechanisms determinately levels off. Sufficiency in general, for every system, cannot be shown by a conceptual study and is an empirical question beyond the study's scope. The result holds for the cases assessed, as completeness does for the elaborations (Method, *Completeness of the elaborations*).

- **The biosphere:** measured by evolutionary activity, the fossil record shows unbounded adaptive activity.[^bedau1998] The biosphere meets every condition, the net beneficial ones included, and shows transformational novelty, so it illustrates that the conditions are compatible with open-ended evolution but does not test their sufficiency. Fossil families also stand in for mechanisms rather than counting them.
- **Lineages in constant environments:** Escherichia coli in a constant environment, whose fitness gains slow,[^wiser2013] and endosymbionts in a constant host, whose genomes shrink,[^mccutcheon2012] lack the environment's share, the endosymbionts in small populations also falling short of reliable transmission, and their novelty declines. Neither counts against sufficiency.
- **Digital organisms:** programs copied by hardware they do not produce, as in Tierra[^ray1991] and Avida,[^lenski2003] are not living systems (Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*), so they are models and cannot count against sufficiency. Read as models, they meet unlimited heredity, information that can be added to, and reliable transmission, the last met by exact copying in the hardware. Tierra's novelty levels off although parasites, hyperparasites, and hosts evolving against them supply change: its world offers only processor time and memory to compete for and no environments to enter, so its coevolution recurs and does not meet the environment's share. Avida's novelty is bounded by the fixed set of tasks it rewards, a fixed environment. Geb, with coevolving agents, was the first to show unbounded activity by the same statistics,[^channon2001] but these count persisting components, not new mechanisms, so whether it meets the criterion is not established. None counts against the conditions as stated, and each supports the environment's share as restated; expansive novelty has been traced to the same factors: several domains of behaviour, bridges between them, and building blocks that combine non-additively.[^taylor2019]
- **Further conditions proposed:** embodiment in chemistry is not a condition, since the conditions are stated by what they achieve, as the criterion for life is (Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*, made systems); what chemistry supplies in every known case, components whose effects open new conditions, falls under the condition that heritable information can specify new mechanisms and under the environment's share (definitional; unconditional). That every known case of open-ended evolution is chemical is co-occurrence, as for the template (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*, *Prerequisites*). The decoupling of heritable records from the components they specify, proposed with autonomy as an enabling condition,[^ruizmirazo2008] corresponds to that condition, and the autonomy to regeneration. Ecological interaction is one way of meeting the environment's share, not a further condition.

What these conditions imply for life's future, with the other patterns the framework sets, is explored speculatively in Life's Future (lifes-future.md).

## Open questions

1. Do the proposed base capabilities and elaborations identify distinct contributions at a useful conceptual grain, or should some be combined, divided, or treated as specialized applications? Closed: the criterion is in Method, *Identifying and naming base capabilities and elaborations* (grain). Applied to each base capability and elaboration, it combines, divides, renames, and reclassifies none; the grounds are in each account's distinctions and minimal realization, and for base capabilities in Method.
2. Which environmental assumptions make each elaboration necessary for continuation, and which conditions can instead be met by the environment? Closed: what the environment can meet is in the environment view's tables and *Conditions only the environment can meet*; the distinction between necessary and net beneficial is in Basis, *Logic of elaboration*; the format for environmental assumptions is in Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*; and each account's minimal realization and *Environmental dependencies and limits* state where it is necessary, where it is a net cost, and where it ceases to suffice. Recombination is necessary only through its share of reliable transmission (see *Recombination*).
3. Are further elaborations missing? Closed: none has been found. The candidates assessed so far sort under existing elaborations, combinations, mechanisms, or changes in what regeneration produces (see *Candidates assessed*), and the procedure for new candidates is in Method, *Completeness of the elaborations*. (Repair of non-heritable components belongs to organizational regeneration; see *Environmental protection*. Modification of the surroundings is not an elaboration; see the environment view, *Observations*. Heat gain was added for the medium and physical range at the components; see *Heat gain*.)
4. Which biological systems and lineages provide useful illustrations or counterexamples to the proposed dependency relations? Closed: none of the cases assessed counts against a relation. The procedure is in Method, *Checks against biology*, and the results are recorded with the relations they test: the dependency view (strictness of the chain); Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems* (viruses, prions, organelles, and other components that propagate and carry variants); the realization view, *Observations* (couplings); *Environmental protection* and *Error correction* (repair drawn by contribution); and Base Capabilities, *Heredity* (unlimited heredity and error correction).
