# LUCA: A Supplement

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This supplement relates the study to current research on the last universal common ancestor (LUCA), the most recent lineage from which all living systems on Earth descend. The two are different kinds of inquiry. LUCA research is historical: it reconstructs what one ancestral lineage had, by comparing the genes of its descendants. The study is conceptual: it asks what a living system, a lineage, or an evolving lineage depends on, and what the environment could meet instead (Method, *Approach*). LUCA is therefore a case the study can be tested against, not a rival account. Like Life's Future, this file adds nothing to the hierarchy or its views; each claim it applies has its home in the main analysis, cited in plain text. The reconstructions it reports are those of the literature, and are themselves revised as methods change.

## What current research reconstructs

Reconstructions infer LUCA's genes from families shared by bacteria and archaea, the two lineages descending from it, after allowing for horizontal transfer between them. An analysis keeping only families with little sign of transfer found 355, and described an anaerobe fixing carbon dioxide by the Wood–Ljungdahl pathway, dependent on hydrogen, and probably living at hydrothermal vents.[^weiss2016] A later probabilistic analysis, counting families with any likelihood of presence, estimated a genome of about 2,600 proteins, comparable to some modern prokaryotes, and dated LUCA to about 4.2 billion years ago, a few hundred million years after Earth formed.[^moody2024] It inferred an anaerobic, acetogenic metabolism, CRISPR-Cas genes, and so viruses, and proposed that LUCA lived in an ecosystem whose other lineages are extinct, its by-products possibly feeding methanogens. Its date and size are disputed: some hold that the early Earth allowed too little time, or that rates of change so early cannot be inferred reliably.

Points of broad agreement are a universal genetic code and ribosome, template copying, a rotary ATP synthase, and enzymes such as pyrophosphatase. Points of dispute include:

- **Membrane.** Archaea and bacteria build membranes from different lipids by unrelated enzymes (the lipid divide), so LUCA's membrane is uncertain; proposals range from a membrane leaky to protons, relying on natural gradients across mineral walls, to a membrane like those of modern cells.[^lombard2012][^sojo2014]
- **Genome and copying.** The main replicative DNA polymerases of bacteria and archaea are not related, which has suggested that DNA replication arose twice, and that LUCA's genome may have been copied differently from either.[^leipe1999]
- **Temperature.** Thermophily is inferred by some analyses and disputed by others, which find a mesophilic LUCA whose descendants adapted to heat in parallel.[^boussau2008]
- **Individual or community.** Woese proposed that before a "Darwinian threshold" genes passed so freely between early cells that the universal ancestor was a community rather than a lineage.[^woese1998][^woese2002]

## LUCA in the study's terms

LUCA is a lineage, not a living system: it names the succession of systems from which present lineages descend. By the reconstructions, it stands at the level of the evolving lineage, with template heredity, and with mechanisms for most elaborations at all three levels.

| Elaboration or base capability | Inferred mechanism in LUCA | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Heredity (template inheritance) | Template copying, with a universal code and ribosome | Broad |
| Error correction | Repair enzymes; required by genome size (see *Genome size and error correction*, below) | Inferred in part |
| Recombination | Recombinases related across domains (RecA/RadA) | Broad |
| Energy transduction | Rotary ATP synthase; Wood–Ljungdahl pathway | Broad; whether driven by its own gradient is disputed |
| Individuation | A membrane of some kind | Disputed |
| Resource provisioning | Membrane transporters | Inferred, with a membrane |
| Waste management | Pyrophosphatase | Broad |
| Environmental protection with responsiveness, carried into new units | CRISPR-Cas | Disputed |
| Self-regulation | Feedback on its own components | Assumed in a genome of this size |
| Heat gain | None: a single cell cannot warm itself (Elaborations, *Heat gain*) | Excluded |
| Dormancy | Not established | Unknown |

CRISPR immunity is not a further elaboration in the study but a combination: environmental protection switched by environmental responsiveness, carried into new units as transgenerational induction (Elaborations, *Combinations at the lineage level*). Its presence implies viruses, whose evolvability is that of components, not heredity (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). By LUCA, then, evolvability of components already ran alongside the heredity of cellular lineages.

## What lies below LUCA

Gene comparison reaches back only to LUCA: whatever preceded it left descendants that are either extinct or merged into its own genes. The stretch between geochemistry and LUCA is invisible to phylogenetics, and it is where the study's minimal realizations sit: regeneration in a single mineral pore, reproduction in a pore field, and heredity by composition, each with the environment meeting every other support condition (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*). The study does not claim that this was the historical route; its order reflects dependencies, not history (Method, *Dependency criterion*). What it supplies is a map of what each step needs and what the surroundings could have met, for a period from which no genes survive. If LUCA lived 4.2 billion years ago with some 2,600 genes, most of the climb from the starting environments to an evolving lineage with most elaborations took place before it, in a few hundred million years or less.

## Debates the framework speaks to

### Living status and dependence on the environment

The 2016 reconstruction was described as "half alive", because it relied on vent geochemistry for energy and perhaps containment. The study rejects this description. Being alive turns on regeneration alone; support conditions met by the environment, such as containment by mineral pores and natural gradients across mineral walls, do not reduce it (Basis, *Organizational regeneration as the proposed criterion for life*). A LUCA dependent on its vent for gradients is a living system whose surroundings meet part of the condition that energy is usable, as in the realization view's treatment of mineral gradients (realization view, *Observations*). What dependence on the environment limits is where the system can continue, not whether it is living.

### Membrane and energy

The study treats energy transduction and individuation as unordered, records their co-occurrence in chemiosmotic cells as a relation of those realizations, and does not make individuation a prerequisite of reproduction or heredity (dependency view, *What this view leaves out, and why*; realization view, *Observations*). A LUCA with a leaky membrane, or with a membrane unlike either descendant's, therefore needs no revision. The lipid divide is evidence about mechanisms: individuation provided by different means in the two descending lineages, as the realization view allows (realization view, *Observations*, one base capability or elaboration provided by different mechanisms). It says nothing against the elaboration having been present.

### Horizontal transfer and the communal ancestor

Heredity needs heritable components to pass mainly from parent to offspring (environment view, *Heredity*), with Woese's Darwinian threshold as the crossover. Where genes pass freely between cells, lineages of cells carry no heredity, and evolvability attaches to the genes as components (Base Capabilities, *Heredity*). This gives the communal-ancestor view a definite reading. Before the threshold, the "ancestor" is a pool of evolvable components within a thin living system, as a lake's community is (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*); after it, the ancestor is a lineage in the study's sense. Near the threshold, whether there was one ancestral lineage is indeterminate, and indeterminacy yields no counterexample to the dependency chain (Method, *Dependency criterion*). The study thus locates the dispute: it is about where a graded threshold was crossed, not about whether LUCA existed.

### LUCA's ecosystem

The proposal that LUCA lived among other lineages, its by-products feeding methanogens, is assessed by the study's account of collectives. Exporting by-products into shared surroundings forms no collective organization; one forms only where something returns to the producer's regeneration, as in cross-feeding (Basis, *Graded and nested living systems*). Where it did, the community was a thin living system, but without collective reproduction it carried no heredity of its own (Basis, *Levels and organizations*, branching). That LUCA's lineage alone survived is the spreading condition failing for every other lineage: their losses were correlated, and only LUCA's descendants were spared (environment view, *Reproduction*).

### Genome size and error correction

The study's error-threshold argument applies whatever LUCA's genome was made of. A genome of some 2.5 million units needs an error rate below about one in 2.5 million per unit, far beyond the 10^4 to 10^5 units that a copying catalyst without correction sustains (Elaborations, *Error correction*, *Prerequisites*). The study therefore implies that LUCA had error correction, a conditional physical relation, if the larger reconstructions are right. The implication bears on the replication puzzle: if LUCA's genome was RNA, as some suggest from the unrelated DNA polymerases, it would need correction far beyond that of the largest RNA viruses, which reach about 41,000 bases with a proofreading exonuclease (Elaborations, *Error correction*, *How the system achieves it*). The smaller 2016 reconstruction eases but does not remove the implication, since its 355 families still exceed what uncorrected copying sustains.

### Temperature

If LUCA was thermophilic, the study classes its tolerance of heat as a change in what regeneration produces, components working at high temperature, not as heat gain, which a single cell cannot achieve (Method, *Heuristic for identifying elaborations*; Elaborations, *Heat gain*). The dispute over LUCA's temperature is therefore a dispute about its components and surroundings, not about which elaborations it had.

## After LUCA: elaborations and mechanisms

Life's Future proposes that novelty is mostly new mechanisms, specialized applications, and combinations rather than new elaborations (Life's Future, *Few new elaborations, many new mechanisms*). LUCA offers a partial test. If the reconstructions are right, most elaborations were present at LUCA, and later evolution added mechanisms for them: new membranes, photosynthetic reaction centers, new protective pigments, motility, and partition machinery. The clear exception is heat gain, which needs large bodies and so arose only in multicellular lineages long after. Dormancy, as in spores, may be another. New collective individuals, the major transitions after LUCA, are recursion, not new levels (Basis, *Levels and organizations*).

## What the comparison cannot show

LUCA research shows what one lineage had; the study asks what any system at a level must have. That LUCA had a feature does not show that the feature is needed, and features shared by every descendant are co-occurrence, which never establishes a dependency (Method, *Testing relations against cases*). Developed systems look as if their elaborations depend on one another because they share mechanisms such as the membrane and ATP (realization view, *Observations*). The main risk in comparing the two is reading LUCA's toolkit as a list of requirements for life. The study's separation of base capabilities, elaborations, and mechanisms is what blocks that reading.

Conversely, the study cannot date or order LUCA's features. Its dependencies constrain possible sequences without establishing an actual one (Method, *Dependency criterion*): heredity cannot precede reproduction, but whether LUCA's ancestors gained a membrane before or after template copying is a historical question the study leaves open.

## Open questions

1. Was the Darwinian threshold crossed before LUCA, so that LUCA is a lineage in the study's sense, or near it, so that its status is indeterminate? This is an empirical question about the balance of vertical and horizontal transmission, which the study's threshold makes precise but cannot settle.
2. Do reconstructions of LUCA include error correction sufficient for the genome size they infer? Agreement would illustrate a conditional physical relation; a large genome reconstructed without correction would call for reassessing either the reconstruction or the relation.
