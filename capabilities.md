# Base Capabilities

*A Functional Hierarchy of Life Processes*

This file gives an account of each base capability, following the format set out in Method, *Format of each account*. Their elaborations are in Elaborations. The relations among base capabilities and elaborations are presented in the views: the dependency view for base capabilities that depend on others, the environment view for the support conditions each base capability needs and what can meet them, and the realization view for the mechanisms that provide each base capability and elaboration.

Examples use the two example systems introduced in the realization view, *Example systems*: the A/B network for primitive examples and a living cell for developed ones.

## Organizational regeneration (proposed criterion for life)

### Added capability

Organizational regeneration is added to persistence, the more elementary way a pattern continues (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). A flame persists only while renewal is supplied from outside it. What regeneration adds is that the means of renewal are themselves products of the pattern's own processes.

An organization-regenerating network is a network of mutually supporting processes whose activity produces and renews the components needed for those processes to continue. In short, it **rebuilds the means of its own continuation**, restoring the organization on which its activity depends.

This is the base capability of the living system. The rationale for using it as a criterion for life is in Basis, *Organizational regeneration as the proposed criterion for life*, and its sufficiency in Basis, *Sufficiency of the criterion*.

### How the system achieves it

A system with organization-regenerating processes maintains its productive organization by renewing essential components as they are consumed, deteriorate, or are lost. The components support processes that, in turn, produce those components. What persists is this organization of mutual support, even as individual components are replaced.

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** Catalyst A helps produce catalyst B from raw materials R, while B helps produce A, in a single mineral pore that confines them, through which R arrives and residue leaves. If production compensates for losses and the catalysts remain available to interact, the network renews the means of its own activity. This illustrates regeneration without assuming the elaborations examined in Elaborations; the necessary support conditions are met by the environment.

**Developed example.** In a living cell, metabolic processes produce catalysts, the membrane, and the genome as components; those components enable the reactions and exchanges needed for further production.

### Environmental dependencies and limits

The environment supplies usable raw materials and energy, together with physical and chemical conditions in which the productive processes can operate. These include suitable temperature and chemical conditions, and sufficient availability of reactants. The system's processes transform these resources, sustaining its organizational regeneration.

For the A/B network, the surroundings supply R, from which A and B can be made, and conditions that permit their production and interaction. The pore keeps the components from dispersing too rapidly. In the cell example, the surroundings supply nutrients and energy sources, while the cell renews its own membrane and productive components.

The environment need not remain perfectly constant, but it must permit regeneration to keep pace with losses over time, with interruptions shorter than the organization survives without renewal; longer interruptions call for dormancy (see Elaborations, *Dormancy*). The contribution described here is organizational regeneration under suitable conditions.

### Prerequisites

No prior base capability or elaboration is assumed. Organizational regeneration is the proposed criterion for life. In the minimal realization considered here, the A/B network in a single mineral pore, the environment meets every support condition: containment, accessible resources, directly usable energy, a medium and physical conditions its chemistry permits, steady inputs keeping component levels within workable proportions, conditions under which damaging influences and by-product accumulation remain tolerable, and conditions without long interruptions. It is the starting environment for the elaborations of the living system (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*).

## Reproduction

### Added capability

Reproduction is added to propagation, the more elementary way a pattern continues across a succession of systems (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). What reproduction adds is that new systems originate from the components and processes of an existing living system.

Reproducing processes produce new living systems from a system's own components. The base capability is to **originate new living systems**, which would otherwise arise only by chance formation in the environment. Reproduction moves continuation from the single system to a lineage, so that an organization can persist even when individual systems do not.

### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example (minimal realization).** The A/B network occupies one of a field of mineral pores linked by narrow channels. Some of its A and B diffuse slowly into a vacant neighboring pore that also receives R. The existing network need only replace what it exports; the new network grows from its founding components until its production matches its losses. Spreading into that pore is reproduction, rather than the enlargement of one system, when:

1. **Origin:** the new network is founded by components from the existing one, not formed by chance.
2. **Own regeneration:** it regenerates its organization from its own production, and would continue if the channel were closed.
3. **Organizational separation:** exchange of A and B between the pores is slow compared with each network's own renewal (Glossary, *System*).

The first two follow from what reproduction and regeneration mean; the third is met here by the pore geometry. Reproduction is completed when the new network's own production comes to outweigh what it receives through the channel; until then it is an extension of the existing network. With the same chemistry, a wide channel gives one system spread across two pores (one system enlarged), and a narrow one gives two systems (reproduction). Separation comes in degrees, and its threshold is relative to renewal (Glossary, *Organizational separation*); near the threshold, the number of systems, and whether reproduction occurred, is indeterminate (see Method, *Dependency criterion*).

In this minimal realization the environment meets each support condition of reproduction (environment view, *Reproduction*): R arriving in the vacant pore supplies the new network's growth; the pore geometry meets separation; chance meets completeness, since the founding set carries many copies of A and B; diffusion into a vacant pore within reach meets room; and pores scattered through the field, with disruptions that do not pass between them, spread the lineage. It is the starting environment for the elaborations of the lineage (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*).

**Combined example.** If the network also produces a jelly (leading to the individuation of a mass), the blob of jelly may grow, and physical disturbance could eventually break it into fragments; each fragment that contains both A and B continues as a new system. Separation is then met by the cohesion of the fragments rather than by the pores. Breaking needs no growth at that moment, but repeated breaking without regrowth would leave fragments too small to keep both A and B.

**Self-localizing example.** In still water, where R diffuses faster than A and B and their production falls short of losses below some density, the network can localize itself as a spot (Elaborations, *Individuation*). With ample R, a spot grows until R is depleted at its center, and it splits in two.[^pearson1993] Each half regenerates from its own production, and little A and B crosses the depleted gap, so separation is met by the production relations and diffusion rates in the still medium, without individuation; chance gives each half a complete set where copies are many.

**Developed example.** In living cells, growth is followed by division. Most bacteria coordinate division with dedicated machinery, but some cells lacking that machinery (bacterial L-forms) can still divide through the physical properties of an overgrown membrane,[^mercier2013] which suggests that division by the system's own means can be very simple. Budding completes separation in a definite event, as when the septum closes between a yeast cell and its bud. In siphonophores, zooids budded from a stem never separate and feed one another through a shared gut, so budding there is growth of one system; the root-connected stems of an aspen clone can remain near the threshold for long periods.

### Environmental dependencies and limits

Reproduction needs growth (Glossary): production in excess of losses. Growth is not needed at each reproduction event. Fragmentation conserves material, so one system can become two without growth at that moment, and starved bacteria undergo reductive divisions, becoming smaller cells. It is needed on average across generations, for two reasons: repeated division without regrowth leaves systems too small to regenerate, and systems lost from the lineage can be replaced only from new material. Where and when the surplus arises varies: in the parent before separation (binary fission), in the parent during separation (budding, in which yeast daughters are born smaller and grow before budding in turn), in the new system after its founding (the pore field above), or accumulated in one phase and spent in successive divisions (multiple fission in Chlamydomonas and, within one organism, the cleavage of an embryo). In L-forms, membrane made faster than the cell's volume grows also drives the division itself.

Reproduction also needs each new unit to receive a complete working set and to reach a site where it can continue, and each new unit needs the support conditions of regeneration at that site (see the environment view, *Reproduction*). Physical forces or the geometry of the surroundings may meet the separation of new units in minimal realizations. The elaborations that can meet these conditions, partitioning and dispersal, are in Elaborations, *Elaborations of the lineage*.

### Prerequisites

- Depends on organizational regeneration (definitional): the new living systems must regenerate their own organization under suitable conditions.
- Does not depend on individuation: the environment can meet the separation of new units, as in the pore field and spot splitting above (see the dependency view, *What this view leaves out, and why*).
- Growth is a support condition, not a prerequisite. Reproduction can occur without it (reductive division), so it fails the dependency criterion. It is needed on average across generations, a physical condition that holds across all realizations within the study's scope, where a system's components come from its own production. It adds no new continuant and no new means: it is regeneration's own production running ahead of losses. It is therefore recorded in the environment view, *Reproduction*, rather than the dependency view.

## Heredity

### Added capability

Heredity is added to evolvability, the more elementary way a succession of systems carries forward particular variants (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*). What heredity adds is that the variant carried forward is a specific organization, regenerated by each new system's own processes.

Heredity preserves the particular organization of a system across reproduction, so that new systems resemble their parent more than other systems in the population. The base capability is to **transmit a specific organization to new systems**. Heritable variation, arising from imperfect transmission, allows lineages to differ, and those differences make selection and diversification possible. Recombination (see Elaborations) adds combinations of variants from different lineages.

### How the system achieves it

**Primitive example.** Suppose the A/B network sometimes produces a variant of catalyst A that is also regenerated by the network. New networks founded from one containing the variant, whether in a neighboring pore or in fragments of jelly, also contain it. The variant stays with its lineage only while it passes mainly from parent to offspring rather than diffusing between pores (see the environment view, *Heredity*). This is inheritance through composition, and it can support only a small number of heritable variants. Compartments that divide without exchange can carry alternative autocatalytic cores as heritable variants, though each carries little heritable information.[^vasas2012] In this minimal realization the environment meets each support condition of heredity (environment view, *Heredity*): slow diffusion between pores keeps transmission mainly vertical and keeps benefits with the variant that produces them; the few variants composition can carry stay within what transmission sustains; and side reactions and disturbance supply variation. It is the starting environment for the elaborations of the evolving lineage, with a template variant where the elaboration needs one (Method, *Stating environmental assumptions*). Where heritable components move freely between compartments, lineages of compartments carry no heredity, and evolvability attaches to the components instead (Basis, *Three levels of continuation for living systems*), as proposed for genetic elements in a field of mineral pores before free-living cells.[^kooninmartin2005]

**Developed example.** In living cells, heredity is based on template copying of DNA, which can support an effectively unlimited number of heritable variants. Discrimination by the copying step itself, as when a polymerase favors correct pairs, belongs to the means of inheritance; a further means that detects and removes errors is error correction (Elaborations, *Error correction*).

### Environmental dependencies and limits

Copying is always imperfect; heredity needs transmission reliable enough for a lineage's particular variants to persist, while variation remains possible. Environmental conditions affect both copying accuracy and which variants persist. Means by which a system improves the accuracy of transmission are in Elaborations, *Error correction*.

### Prerequisites

- Depends on reproduction (definitional): transmission across generations presupposes generations.
- Template copying is not a definitional prerequisite of heredity; compositional inheritance is chemically possible.
- **Unlimited heredity** (vastly more possible heritable variants than systems in the population) needs many possible variants, not a large amount of heritable information. A template of four kinds of unit about 60 units long allows some 10^36 variants, more than the roughly 10^30 prokaryotes on Earth.[^whitman1998] Enzyme-free copying, by the inherent selectivity of the chemistry, sustains a few tens to about a hundred units, and a produced copying catalyst without correction, whose selectivity also belongs to the means of inheritance, about 10^4 to 10^5 (Elaborations, *Error correction*). Unlimited heredity therefore needs a template of modest length, but not error correction.
- A template is not established as physically necessary for unlimited heredity. Every known case uses modular inheritance (Glossary, *Heredity*): templates, epigenetic marks placed by position along DNA, and sequences of symbols in culture, which are heredity where carried by collective heredity and otherwise the evolvability of components (Basis, *Levels and organizations*). A physical argument favors it: in modular inheritance possible variants multiply with length without new chemistry, whereas each heritable state of a compositional network needs interactions of its own, and model compositional networks carry few heritable variants and transmit them weakly.[^vasas2010][^vasas2012][^szathmary2000] But one proposed dissociation has not failed on physical grounds: a network of many independent switches, each held in one of two states by its own feedback and inherited by composition. By Method, *Testing relations against cases*, this is co-occurrence with a supporting argument, not a physical dependency (ground physical and scope unconditional, were it established).
- What open-ended evolution needs of heredity is set out in Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*, the study's one home for those questions.

## Open questions

1. Does open-ended evolution need more heritable information than the selectivity of copying sustains, and so error correction? Is a template physically necessary for unlimited heredity? Closed: open-ended evolution needs heritable information that can be added to, and so error correction once that information exceeds about one over the copying step's error rate per unit, a conditional physical relation (Elaborations, *Extended endpoint: open-ended evolution*). A template is not established as physically necessary for unlimited heredity; every known case uses modular inheritance (see *Heredity*, *Prerequisites*).
